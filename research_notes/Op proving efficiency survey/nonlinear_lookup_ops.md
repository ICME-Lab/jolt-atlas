## Rsqrt: how it is defined and proven today, and whether an algebraic constraint or a small table would be cheaper

### Takeaway
Rsqrt is `⌊√⌊S³/x⌋⌋`, proven with one degree-3 sumcheck plus two 64-bit-address `UnsignedLessThan` lookups, for 33 committed polynomials per node (1 dense quotient + 2×16 one-hot chunks) and RaVirtual checks of degree 17. A bit-identical single relation `out²·x ≤ S³ < (out+1)²·x` (the zkGPT pattern) would remove the committed quotient and one remainder, and proving the two slacks with the repo's existing "value range check" would remove both read-raf sumchecks and both RaVirtual checks; in transformer models Rsqrt nodes are tiny, so the gain is in polynomial and instance count rather than committed volume.

### Cited Findings
- **[CODE]** Definition: `rsqrt(x, scale)` returns 0 for `x ≤ 0`, otherwise `(2^(3·scale) / x).isqrt()` in i64 — `atlas-trace/src/semantics/fixed.rs:58-65`.
- **[CODE]** The relation is `eq(r₀, r′)·(x̂·q + r_div − S³ + γ·(out² + r_sqrt − q))`, input claim 0, `log_T` rounds, degree 3 — `jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/ops/rsqrt.rs:121-200` (degree at `:185`).
- **[CODE]** Committed polynomials per node: `RsqrtQuotient` (dense) plus `d` each of `SqrtDivRangeCheckRaD` and `SqrtRangeCheckRaD`, where `d` comes from the range-check encoding — `rsqrt.rs:61-73`. The range-check encoding's `log_k()` is `LOG_K = 64` (`jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/range_checking/mod.rs:247`), so `d = 16` and the total is 1 + 32 = 33.
- **[CODE]** The two remainders are virtual (`VirtualPoly::DivRemainder`, `VirtualPoly::SqrtRemainder`), bound through the read-raf's operand claims; the bounds are `r_div < x̂` and `r_sqrt < 2·out + 1`, each one lookup into `UnsignedLessThanTable<32>` on an interleaved (remainder, bound) 64-bit address — `rsqrt.rs:244-275`, `jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/range_checking/range_check_operands.rs` (`RiRangeCheckOperands`, `RsRangeCheckOperands`, and `transform_operand_claims` for `2·v̂+1`).
- **[CODE]** Both range-check read-rafs go into the model-wide deferred `RangeCheck` batch, and their one-hot checks into a following batch — `rsqrt.rs:256,264`; `jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/deferred_lookups.rs:45-49`.
- **[CODE]** Non-positive inputs are outside the relation's domain; "the division range check rejects them" — `rsqrt.rs:146-152`.
- **[CODE]** Every `atlas.Rsqrt` comes from LayerNorm fusion (`MeanOfSquares → Add eps → Rsqrt → Mul`) or the `Reciprocal(Sqrt(v))` rewrite — `atlas-onnx-import/src/passes.rs:385-395, 596-603, 700-712`. Its input is the per-row variance, so `T` is the number of normalized rows (sequence positions), not the hidden size.
- **[LIT]** zkGPT proves division and square root each with "a single range relation": for division `C₂·q_y·(2q_z − 1) ≤ 2C₁·q_x < C₂·q_y·(2q_z + 1)`, and "similarly, square root can be transformed into one range relation" — [zkGPT, USENIX Security 2025](https://home.cse.ust.hk/~kaichen/papers/zkgpt-security25.pdf).
- **[LIT]** zkGPT's "constraint fusion" merges mean, variance and square root of the normalization layer into one range relation, `n(2q_σ − 1)² ≤ Σ(n·q_xi − Σq_xi)² < n(2q_σ + 1)²`, replacing three range relations used by prior work; it states that "proving range relations is the bottleneck in our system, as it is more costly than proving arithmetic relations" and proves range relations with Lasso — [zkGPT](https://home.cse.ust.hk/~kaichen/papers/zkgpt-security25.pdf).
- **[LIT]** zkLLM handles LayerNorm's "compound non-arithmetic operations of square-root and inverse" with "two sequential tlookup steps", the first a verifiable downscaling of the input "to reduce the overall sizes of the lookup tables", the second the quantized compound operation — [zkLLM, arXiv 2404.16109](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16109).
- **[LIT]** DeepProve states that GELU and LayerNorm in GPT-2 are proven through table-based (lookup) representations; a later update replaced separate per-operation implementations with one parameterized Lookup layer — [Lagrange engineering update, July 2025](https://lagrange.dev/engineering-updates/july-2025), [Q4 2025 update](https://lagrange.dev/engineering-updates/lagrange-engineering-update-q4-2025), [DeepProve ePrint 2026/1112](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1112).
- **[LIT]** The Jolt Atlas paper lists Rsqrt as a supported math op and describes division as a non-deterministic operation carrying an advice value; it does not describe a dedicated rsqrt protocol — [Jolt Atlas, arXiv 2602.17452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17452) (same text as `papers/Jolt_Atlas_Whitepaper.pdf`).
- **[LIT]** ZKTorch proves Div/Mod with the ZKML (Chen et al. 2024) technique, batching the checks across a tensor and range-checking remainders with a cq lookup — [ZKTorch, arXiv 2507.07031](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07031v2).

### Inferences
- **[EST] Cost per Rsqrt node today:** 33 committed polynomials; 2 lookups per element; one degree-3 sumcheck of `log_T` rounds; two degree-2 read-rafs of `64 + log_T` rounds; two RaVirtual checks of degree 17 over `log_T` rounds; two Booleanity checks of degree 3 over `4 + log_T` rounds.
- **[EST] Soundness of the current protocol looks self-contained:** `q` is a committed field element with no range check of its own, but `out² + r_sqrt = q` with both `2·out+1` and `r_sqrt` confined to 32-bit lookup operands forces `q` to be a small integer, after which `x̂·q + r_div = S³` cannot wrap modulo the 128-bit field. I reasoned this through; I did not test it.
- **[EST] Proposal R1 — single-relation rsqrt, bit-identical output.** Because `⌊√⌊y⌋⌋ = ⌊√y⌋` for real `y ≥ 0`, the current semantics are equivalent to `out²·x ≤ S³ < (out+1)²·x`. Witness two non-negative slacks `a = S³ − out²·x` and `b = (out+1)²·x − 1 − S³`, both linear in already-opened quantities once `out²·x` is opened. This needs no `SemVersion` bump, removes the dense `RsqrtQuotient` commitment and the division remainder, and raises the relation to degree 4 (eq · out · out · x). Bit widths: `(2·out+1)·x ≈ 2·√(S³·x) ≤ 2^37.5` at scale 14 with `x < 2^31`, so each slack fits 38–40 bits, i.e. 10 chunks each.
- **[EST] Proposal R2 — prove the slacks with the existing value range check instead of `UnsignedLessThan`.** `clamp_split.rs` already proves `R < 2^width` for fused-rescale remainders with a value sumcheck plus a Booleanity linear term, with no read-raf and no RaVirtual (`jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/clamp_split.rs:1-56`, `global_clamp.rs:87-110`). Applied to R1's two slacks: about 20 chunks instead of 32, zero read-raf rounds instead of `2·(64 + log_T)`, and no degree-17 RaVirtual. Packing every Rsqrt node of a model into one bucket (as `global_clamp::pack_buckets` does) makes the polynomial count roughly 20 per model instead of 33 per node.
- **[EST] Leading-zero normalization plus a mantissa table is not attractive here.** Under one-hot chunking the cost is the number of address bits. Normalization needs the input decomposed (8 chunks for 32 bits), a mantissa lookup (3–4 chunks) and a verified output shift with its own remainder check; that is no fewer chunks than R1+R2 and it changes the output values (new `SemVersion`, new golden hashes).
- **[EST] Newton-step or tolerance-based verification (accept any `y` with `|y²·x − S³|` small) is the same relation as R1 with a looser bound.** A loose bound makes the output non-unique, which conflicts with the repo invariant that the verifier never trusts the prover for anything the trace semantics define (CLAUDE.md, "Prover/Verifier Invariants"). The exact-floor bound in R1 keeps uniqueness.
- **[EST] zkGPT-style fusion of MeanOfSquares + Rsqrt** would remove the MoS remainder range check too (16 more one-hot chunks per LayerNorm today, `CommittedPoly::MeanOfSquaresRangeCheckRaD`), but it changes rounding of the intermediate variance and therefore the output bits; it needs a new `SemVersion` and an accuracy check.

### Gaps
- No timing for Rsqrt on the current Fp128+Akita backend exists in the repo. The only per-family numbers are from the retired BN254/Dory backend (see the cost-dominance section).
- Rsqrt node count and `T` for GPT-2/Qwen/BGE were not measured (would require running `dump_lowered_ops`, a cargo command). A user-local memory note from Aug 2026 reports "MoS/Sqrt RC 2,352 polys" on Qwen, consistent with 48 one-hot polynomials per LayerNorm × 49 norms, but that predates the current code.
- I could not retrieve DeepProve's technical description of its LayerNorm table (the engineering post returned HTTP 403 and the ePrint landing page has only the abstract), so the table size and input range reduction it uses are unknown.
- zkGPT's square-root relation is stated only as "similarly"; the exact inequality was not in the text I extracted (the extracted PDF text was partial).

## Div and scalar_const_div: how the range checks are done and whether they can be batched or shared

### Takeaway
Div commits a dense quotient plus 16 one-hot chunks and proves `rem < divisor` with a per-node (not deferred) 64-bit `UnsignedLessThan` lookup; ScalarConstDiv commits a dense remainder and has no range check at all, which the code itself documents as a soundness gap. A shared range-check mechanism already exists in the repo (packed remainder buckets), and LogUp-GKR was built and measured once on the old BN254 backend as a net regression, so the cheapest path is to route these checks through the existing buckets rather than add a new argument.

### Cited Findings
- **[CODE]** Div relation: `eq(r₀, r′)·(right·q + rem − left)`, input claim 0, `log_T` rounds, degree 3 — `jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/ops/div.rs:109-165` (degree at `:153`).
- **[CODE]** Div commits `DivNodeQuotient` (dense) and, for non-scalar nodes, `d = 16` `DivRangeCheckRaD` chunks — `div.rs:43-53`. The quotient is tied to the node's reduced output by a zero-round "tie" relation (`DivQuotientRelation`, `div.rs:92, 210-262`).
- **[CODE]** Div's range check and one-hot checks are verified inline in the node's turn (`verify_relation` on `ProofType::RangeCheck`, then a 2-instance batch on `ProofType::RaOneHotChecks`), not in the deferred batches — `div.rs:283-327`. Rsqrt and MeanOfSquares do use `DeferredBatch::RangeCheck`.
- **[CODE]** Semantics: `floor_div` with the remainder taking the divisor's sign — `atlas-trace/src/semantics/fixed.rs:33-55`; the prover's `adjusted_remainder` mirrors it (`jolt-atlas-verifier/src/utils/mod.rs:24-30`).
- **[CODE]** ScalarConstDiv relation: input claim `d·q(r₀)`, output `eq(r₀, r′)·(left − rem)`, degree 2, one committed dense `ScalarConstDivNodeRemainder`. The doc comment states: "Nothing bounds the committed remainder, so the relation does not pin `q` to the floor: a known soundness gap of this op's protocol, which the relation preserves" — `jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/ops/scalar_const_div.rs:31-33, 62-73, 107`.
- **[CODE]** The importer emits ScalarConstDiv only as `atlas.Descale` with divisor `2^scale`, for float→int/bool casts — `atlas-onnx-import/src/lower.rs:255-260`, `atlas-onnx-import/src/passes.rs:1393-1396, 1458`. A constant-divisor ONNX `Div` is rewritten to a multiply by the reciprocal — `passes.rs:605-612`.
- **[CODE]** Shared range checks already exist for fused-rescale remainders: nodes of one rebase width are packed into buckets with one set of committed chunks and one value sumcheck + Booleanity per bucket; "one set of committed chunks and one set of checks whatever the node count" — `jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/global_clamp.rs:1-18, 87-110`; verification in `deferred_lookups.rs:272-312`. Only Einsum/Mul/Square/Cube qualify today (`global_clamp.rs:87-94`).
- **[CODE]** Akita commits dense polynomials under a "dense-bounded flavor" that "admits coefficients in `[0, 2^64)`", with signed classes shifted by a public offset — `atlas-pcs/src/akita.rs:19-25`.
- **[LIT]** LogUp-GKR (Papini and Haböck, ePrint 2023/1284) removes the need to commit to the helper polynomials of the original log-derivative lookup by evaluating the fractional sum with GKR — [summary of LogUp and cq](https://georgwiese.github.io/crypto-summaries/Concepts/Protocols/Lookup-Arguments/LogUp--and--cq).
- **[LIT]** Shout commits only one-hot read addresses, avoids grand-product/grand-sum arguments, and when the address is split into `d` chunks the commitment key shrinks to `K^(1/d)` while per-variable degree rises from 3 to `2 + d` — [Twist and Shout, ePrint 2025/105](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/105); [summary](https://georgwiese.github.io/crypto-summaries/Papers/Twist-and-Shout).
- **[LIT]** zkLLM's tlookup is a sumcheck-based log-derivative lookup over tensors; its experiments use tlookups "each … having a size of 2^16", and fuse rescaling into the following activation — [zkLLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16109).
- **[LIT]** DeepProve uses LogUp-GKR lookups over decomposable tables for nonlinear operators — [Inside DeepProve (search summary)](https://lagrange.dev/engineering-updates/inside-deepprove-proving-an-llm-end-to-end).
- **[CODE-adjacent, historic]** A user-local memory note (Aug 2026, BN254/Dory backend) records that LogUp-GKR range checks were built and measured: "NET REGRESSION 46.3 → 55.4 s. GKR fractional sum costs ~30-60 BN254 mults/element vs one-hot's ~8 batched affine adds + few mults … LogUp only pays on small fields" — `/Users/kg/.claude/projects/-Users-kg-swe-icme-official-jolt-atlas/memory/qwen-sub-minute-brainstorm.md`. This is not in the repo and predates Fp128/Akita.

### Inferences
- **[EST] Div cost per node today:** 17 committed polynomials (1 dense + 16 one-hot); 1 lookup per element; a degree-3 sumcheck of `log_T` rounds; a degree-2 read-raf of `64 + log_T` rounds; RaVirtual of degree 17; Booleanity of degree 3.
- **[EST] ScalarConstDiv cost per node today:** 1 dense committed polynomial; 0 lookups; one degree-2 sumcheck of `log_T` rounds. It is cheap because it is incomplete.
- **[EST] Proposal D1 — close the ScalarConstDiv gap through the remainder buckets.** The divisor is always `2^scale`, so `rem ∈ [0, 2^scale)` is exactly the check the remainder buckets already prove. Make the remainder a bucket value (virtual, defined by its chunks) and drop the dense commitment: 1 dense column becomes `ceil(14/4) = 4` one-hot chunks shared with any other 14-bit bucket. This costs more than today, but today's protocol is unsound; alternatively the importer can remove the op by folding the descale into the producer's fused rebase where the producer is a rebasing op.
- **[EST] Proposal D2 — Div via two value range checks.** Replace `rem <ᵤ divisor` on a 64-bit interleaved address with `rem ∈ [0, 2^w)` and `divisor − 1 − rem ∈ [0, 2^w)` in the bucket machinery. At `w = 32` this is still 16 chunks, but with no read-raf (`64 + log_T` rounds saved) and no degree-17 RaVirtual, and it becomes poolable across nodes. Negative divisors need a sign case that the current unsigned-less-than encoding also has to handle; I did not trace how it does.
- **[EST] Div quotient range.** `q` is opened from a dense commitment and the relation is a field identity; unlike Rsqrt nothing inside the protocol forces `q` to be a small integer. If Akita's dense-bounded commitment soundly confines coefficients to `[0, 2^64)` after the signed shift, then `right·q + rem` cannot wrap (`< 2^97`) and the relation is sound; otherwise a non-integer field element `q = (left − rem)/right` would satisfy it. No `known_gap_*` test pins this (`grep known_gap jolt-atlas-core/src` shows only neg/gather/broadcast/qwen pins). This needs confirmation by someone who knows Akita's extraction guarantee.
- **[EST] A global LogUp-GKR range check is not obviously better on this stack.** It needs the checked value committed densely (or virtual with an opening) plus a committed multiplicity vector, and its prover does field inversions/multiplications per element in a 128-bit field; the one-hot path commits 0/1 values and its Booleanity is heavily tuned. The one historic measurement went against LogUp. What changed since is the backend: on Akita the commit phase is 62% of GPT-2 proving time (README), so the trade should be re-measured rather than assumed either way.

### Gaps
- Whether Akita's "dense-bounded" flavor gives a sound coefficient bound (and with what slack) was not established; I read only the adapter's module doc, not `akita-*` upstream. If it does, dense commitments carry a free 64-bit range check and several one-hot range checks could be replaced by dense columns.
- How remainder buckets enforce widths that are not multiples of 4 (e.g. 14-bit rebase with 4 chunks spanning 16 bits) was not traced; `one_hot_params(width)` uses `div_ceil`. Worth a targeted review before reusing buckets for new checks.
- How often `Op::Div` appears in the zoo models was not measured.
- I could not fetch the Twist and Shout PDF (HTTP 403), so its per-lookup field-operation counts are not quoted.

## Activation tables (tanh, erf, sigmoid, sin, cos, GELU via erf; ReLU): table size, decomposition, and whether clamping, smaller tables, piecewise polynomials or symmetry cut cost

### Takeaway
Tanh/Erf/Sigmoid each cost 13 committed one-hot chunks and 2 lookups per element: 8 chunks for a 32-bit clamp lookup that only reduces the input to 18 bits, and 5 chunks for the 2^18 table itself. The clamp half can be removed by reusing the table's own address chunks as the clamp's range proof (the interior/saturated split the repo already uses for saturating clamps), which would cut the dense one-hot work per element from 13 chunks to 5; table-structure tricks (symmetry, piecewise polynomials) do not reduce per-lookup cost in a one-hot argument, only narrower addresses do.

### Cited Findings
- **[CODE]** Table definition: tanh/sigmoid/erf use `IndexRule::SymClamp { bound_log: scale + 3 }`, i.e. inputs are clamped to `[−2^(s+3), 2^(s+3))` (±8.0) and the table has `2^(s+4)` entries, one per quantized input; at scale 14 that is 2^18 — `atlas-trace/src/semantics/tables.rs:40-66, 118-127`; `common/src/consts/activation.rs:5-8`.
- **[CODE]** Entries are computed in integer arithmetic from Q2.62 approximations; `erf_entry` saturates to `±S` for `|x| ≥ 4S` — `tables.rs:130-164`.
- **[CODE]** A clamped activation is two lookup stages: (1) `output = SmallTable[clamped]`, a small-table read-raf with `ACTIVATION_TABLE_VARS = 18` rounds, degree 2, γ-batched with a signed identity so the address equals `clamped`; (2) `clamped = ActivationClampTable[raw_input]`, a prefix-suffix read-raf with `LOG_K = XLEN = 32` — `jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/ops/activation_clamped/mod.rs:1-14, 46, 73, 115, 190-213`.
- **[CODE]** Committed polynomials per activation node: `clamp_d` `ActivationClampRaD` + `small_d` `ActivationSmallRaD` — `activation_clamped/mod.rs:221-232`; with 32 and 18 address bits that is 8 + 5 = 13. No dense column is committed.
- **[CODE]** Both stages and their one-hot checks are deferred into model-wide batches (`ActivationExec`, `ActivationClamp`) — `activation_clamped/mod.rs:199-218`; `deferred_lookups.rs:45-49`. They are not packed into shared buckets.
- **[CODE]** Sin/Cos: reduce the input modulo `round(k·2π·2^scale)`, right-shift by `TRIG_DOWNSCALE_BITS` so the table never exceeds 16 vars, then look up — `atlas-trace/src/semantics/fixed.rs:69-105`, `tables.rs:207-240`. Committed: `TeleportNodeQuotient` (dense) + `TrigDownscaleRaD` (32-bit `RightShiftTable`, 8 chunks) + `SinRaD`/`CosRaD` (table vars / 4) + `TeleportRangeCheckRaD` (64-bit, 16 chunks) — `jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/ops/sin.rs:32-46`. The three read-rafs are one per-node batch and the three one-hot pairs another (`sin.rs:64-128`). The input claim is recovered as `τ·q + r` with no sumcheck (`neural_teleport/division.rs`). Verification rejects any scale other than `MODEL_SCALE` (`sin.rs:54-60`).
- **[CODE]** ReLU: one prefix-suffix lookup into `ReluTable<32>`, 8 `NodeOutputRaD` chunks, then the two one-hot checks, all inline per node — `jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/ops/relu.rs:18-68`.
- **[CODE]** The importer maps ONNX `Tanh`/`Sigmoid`/`Erf`/`Sin`/`Cos`/`Relu` one-to-one and has no GELU fusion pass (no match for "gelu" in `atlas-onnx-import/src/passes.rs`) — `atlas-onnx-import/src/lower.rs:242-247`. GELU's surrounding Mul/Add nodes are therefore separate ops with their own clamp and remainder checks.
- **[CODE]** The saturating-clamp split: output chunks prove `OUT ∈ [0, 2^32)`, a virtual indicator `IND` is the Hamming weight of sparse "slack" chunks, and one split sumcheck plus Booleanity with a linear term proves `out = SatClamp(acc)`; "no RaVirtual (the most expensive check), the dense chunk count drops from `w/4` to 8, and the `w/4` slack chunks are almost empty" — `jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/clamp_split.rs:1-56`.
- **[LIT]** The Jolt Atlas paper's teleportation: replace `σ(x)` by `σ(x/τ)` with a global `τ`, a lossy approximation; "with τ = 4 … the lookup table is bounded by 2^16 entries"; future work proposes choosing `τ` jointly with the prefix-suffix chunk parameters — [Jolt Atlas, arXiv 2602.17452](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17452v1).
- **[LIT]** "In a Shout-style (one-hot) lookup argument the per-lookup proving work is a function of the access pattern alone, never of the table values"; function structure can only reduce a secondary once-per-proof table term; the one free parameter is the committed address width, and tuning it gave "a bit-exact reduction in prover time that has been upstreamed", measured on EZKL/halo2 and Jolt Atlas — [A Separation Principle for Lookup-Based zkML, ePrint 2026/1390](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1390) (abstract only read).
- **[LIT]** zkGPT replaces GELU by a piecewise form "z-GeLU": `G = (g + |g|)/2 − 1_{|g| ≥ th}·g·(a·|g|² + b·|g| + c)`, a threshold plus a quadratic, proven with range relations rather than a function table — [zkGPT](https://home.cse.ust.hk/~kaichen/papers/zkgpt-security25.pdf).
- **[LIT]** zkLLM approximates `GELU(z) ≈ z·Sigmoid(1.702z)` and reduces Sigmoid to its zkAttn (digit-decomposed exponent) machinery to avoid a table over all input-output pairs; zkAttn used `K = 5` tlookups of size 2^16 — [zkLLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16109).
- **[LIT]** ZKTorch replaces the ONNX GELU subgraph (multiplications, a Pow and a Tanh) with a single node proven by one table lookup, "reducing CQs from 6 to 1" — [ZKTorch](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07031v2).
- **[LIT]** A CCS 2025 paper characterizes piecewise-polynomial activation approximations as reducing circuit size but adding approximation error when used alone — [ZIP, CCS 2025](https://thanghoang.github.io/publication/25_ccs_zip/25_ccs_zip.pdf) (search snippet only).

### Inferences
- **[EST] Tanh/Erf/Sigmoid cost per node today:** 13 one-hot chunk polynomials; 2 lookups per element; read-raf A of 18 rounds degree 2 over a materialized 2^18 table; read-raf B of `32 + log_T` rounds degree 2; RaVirtual of degree 6 (small table) and degree 9 (clamp); two Booleanity checks of degree 3.
- **[EST] Sin/Cos cost per node today (scale 14):** I recomputed the constants from the code's search: `k = 24`, modulus 2,470,649, natural table 22 bits, downshift 6, table 16 vars. So 1 dense + 8 + 4 + 16 = 29 committed polynomials and 3 lookups per element; RaVirtual degrees 9, 5 and 17.
- **[EST] ReLU cost per node today:** 8 one-hot chunks; 1 lookup per element; read-raf of `32 + log_T` rounds degree 2; RaVirtual degree 9; Booleanity degree 3.
- **[EST] Proposal A1 — fuse the activation clamp into the table lookup (highest-value item for GELU-heavy models).** The 5 small-table chunks already prove `clamped ∈ [−2^17, 2^17)`. What remains is `clamped = clamp(x)`, which is the clamp-split statement with an 18-bit output: `x − clamped = IND·S·sign`, `IND ∈ {0,1}`, `IND = 1 ⇒ clamped ∈ {min, max}`, with `S` in sparse slack chunks that are empty wherever the input is inside ±8.0. Per element the dense one-hot work drops from 13 chunks to 5 (about −62%), lookups from 2 to 1, the `32 + log_T`-round read-raf and the degree-9 RaVirtual disappear, and with the existing per-bucket "exact" flag the slack chunks need not be opened when nothing saturates. Output bits are unchanged, so no `SemVersion` bump. Changes: verifier/prover `activation_clamped`, a new relation plus its `S ⊆ R` note, new `mutate::Site`s; tracer and importer untouched. Soundness caveat: the argument that pointwise identities follow from random `r_n, γ, β` must be redone for the signed 18-bit output, including the sign of the slack on each saturated side.
- **[EST] Proposal A2 — pack activation lookups into model-wide buckets** as clamps and remainders are. This does not change committed volume but collapses per-node polynomial and sumcheck-instance counts (13 per node today) into a per-model constant, which is what reduced the opening-reduction instance count for clamps.
- **[EST] Smaller tables by tighter clamping save little.** Chunk count is `ceil(bits/4)`: 18 bits and 17 bits both need 5 chunks. Erf's table is exactly saturated beyond ±4.0 by construction, so a 17-bit erf table would be bit-identical, but it saves no chunk; it only halves the once-per-node 2^18 table evaluation. Reaching 16 bits (4 chunks, −20% of A1's remaining 5) requires dropping input precision (the paper's `τ`), which changes outputs. By my arithmetic tanh at scale 14 is not saturated until about ±5.5 and sigmoid not until about ±10.4, so their ±8 clamp is already part of the function definition.
- **[EST] Odd/reflection symmetry does not help per lookup.** Halving the table via `T[−x] = −T[x]` leaves 17 address bits (still 5 chunks) and adds a sign-handling relation. This matches the separation-principle claim.
- **[EST] Piecewise-polynomial approximation with a segment lookup is not cheaper in this cost model.** A segment index of 12 bits (3 chunks) plus a 6-bit offset (2 chunks) plus the rounding remainder of the interpolation (2 chunks) is 7 chunks and a higher-degree relation, against 5 chunks for the direct 18-bit table after A1. Piecewise forms pay off in systems where a table lookup costs in proportion to table size or where range relations are the unit of cost (zkGPT), not where cost is address bits.
- **[EST] Proposal A3 — GELU fusion in the importer.** Following ZKTorch, recognize the erf-GELU and tanh-GELU subgraphs and emit one `Gelu` table op: `y = GELU(clamp(x))` as a single 18-bit lookup with a linear tail outside the clamp. This removes the surrounding Mul/Add/Cube nodes with their saturating-clamp and remainder checks, which are outside this note's op list but are where GELU's cost actually multiplies. It changes output bits (one rounding instead of several), so it needs a new table kind, a `SemVersion` bump, re-pinned golden hashes, and an accuracy run. The tail is the catch: GELU is not saturating (`GELU(x) → x`), so the op must define `y = x` above the clamp and `y = 0` below it, which the clamp-split slack term can express.
- **[EST] Sin/Cos improvement.** The 16-chunk teleport range check `r < τ` has a constant bound, so two value range checks of 22 bits (6 chunks each) replace 16 chunks, one read-raf and a degree-17 RaVirtual; the right-shift lookup (8 chunks) can become a chunk-aligned split of `r` if the downshift is made a multiple of 4 (e.g. 8 instead of 6, table 14 bits), which would change output bits. Trig ops are absent from the transformer zoo as far as the importer passes show, so this ranks low.
- **[EST] ReLU improvement.** `out = max(x, 0)` can be proven with a sign bit and magnitude chunks instead of a 32-bit prefix-suffix table: same 8 chunks, but no `32 + log_T`-round read-raf and no degree-9 RaVirtual. Low priority for transformer models.

### Gaps

## What dominates per-lookup cost: committing one-hot ra polynomials, the read-raf sumcheck, booleanity/hamming checks, or something else

### Takeaway
On the current Akita backend the only published breakdown is whole-prover: commitment is 25.2 s of 40.6 s for GPT-2, the IOP 5.3 s and openings 6.7 s, so committed one-hot volume is the first-order cost and sumcheck degree/rounds second. There is no per-op or per-lookup-family timing for the current backend anywhere in the repo.

### Cited Findings
- **[CODE]** README benchmark, GPT-2 on a MacBook Pro M3 with Akita: witness + commitment 25.2 s, IOP 5.28 s, opening reduction + Akita openings 6.72 s, total prove 40.6 s, verify 2.21 s; untraced median of 3 runs 39.9 s prove, 2.42 MiB proof, 6.2 GB peak memory; the retired Dory backend proved the same model in 26.7 s — `README.md:59-82`.
- **[CODE]** Akita commits one-hot polynomials sparsely, up to 64 K=16 columns per class, and one-hot polynomials narrower than 16 variables are packed densely instead; there is no additive homomorphism, so each class is opened natively — `atlas-pcs/src/akita.rs:4-33, 79-89`.
- **[CODE]** The clamp-split design note calls RaVirtual "the most expensive check" among the lookup sumchecks — `jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/clamp_split.rs:53-55`.
- **[CODE]** The deferred-lookup module doc records why batching was introduced: about 5 sumchecks × about 750 nodes, each with 64+ rounds "whose per-round work is far too small to amortize the per-round overhead" — `jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/deferred_lookups.rs:1-18`.
- **[CODE, historic]** A Chrome trace at the repo root (`trace-1792493663983876.json`, dated Oct 20, Dory/BN254, a 2,172-node model, 50.3 s `ONNXProof::prove`) aggregates to: `commit_witness_polynomials` 17.1 s, `iop` 17.1 s, `prove_reduced_openings` 10.2 s, `deferred_lookups::prove_all` 9.7 s; summed over threads, `BooleanitySumcheckProver::compute_message` 7.4 s, `InstructionReadRafSumcheckProver::ingest_challenge` 6.5 s, `RaSumcheckProver::compute_message` 6.3 s, `OneHotPolynomialProverOpening::initialize` 13.0 s, and 24 `Sigmoid::prove` spans 0.48 s. I aggregated begin/end events myself; nested and parallel spans overlap, so these are not additive.
- **[CODE-adjacent, historic]** The user-local memory note (BN254/Dory, Qwen) records 18,106 committed polynomials, "~all one-hot", before optimization, and that removing RaVirtual and the identity range check for remainders took the total from 70.2 s to 66.5 s — `/Users/kg/.claude/projects/-Users-kg-swe-icme-official-jolt-atlas/memory/qwen-sub-minute-brainstorm.md`.
- **[LIT]** In Shout, splitting the address into `d` chunks trades a smaller commitment key for more commitments per lookup and a higher sumcheck degree (`2 + d`) — [Twist and Shout summary](https://georgwiese.github.io/crypto-summaries/Papers/Twist-and-Shout).
- **[LIT]** zkGPT reports range relations, not arithmetic relations, as its bottleneck — [zkGPT](https://home.cse.ust.hk/~kaichen/papers/zkgpt-security25.pdf).

### Inferences
- **[EST]** With commitment at about 62% of GPT-2 proving time on Akita, the most reliable lever is the number of one-hot chunk entries committed per element. Per element the ops in scope commit: Tanh/Erf/Sigmoid 13 chunks, Div 16 + 1 dense, Rsqrt 32 + 1 dense, Sin/Cos 28 + 1 dense, ReLU 8, ScalarConstDiv 1 dense.
- **[EST]** The second lever is RaVirtual, whose degree is `d + 1`: 17 for every 64-bit range check, 9 for 32-bit lookups, 6 for the activation table. Value-style range checks avoid it entirely.
- **[EST]** The third lever is instance and polynomial count for small nodes (Rsqrt, MoS, activations): read-raf instances of `64 + log_T` rounds dominate when `T` is tiny, and each committed polynomial is an opening-reduction input. Bucket packing addresses this without changing committed volume.
- **[EST]** The historic numbers cannot be transferred to Akita: Dory's cost was pairing- and MSM-bound, and the old field was 254-bit. In particular the earlier conclusions "chunk width changes don't help" and "LogUp-GKR is a regression" were about that backend.

### Gaps
- No per-op, per-family or per-stage trace for Fp128+Akita exists in the repo (`docs/`, `specs/`, README, `reports/` is empty). A single `--trace-terminal` run of GPT-2 and of a GELU-via-erf model (BGE) would settle the ranking below; I was instructed not to run cargo.
- The relative Akita cost of one dense bounded column versus one K=16 one-hot column of the same length is unknown, and it decides whether "dense value + cheap range argument" designs can beat one-hot chunks.
- CI logs were not consulted (no network access to them from here).

## Ranked proposals for this codebase (objective c)

### Takeaway
Ranked by expected saving per unit of risk: (1) fuse the activation clamp into the table lookup, (2) move every `UnsignedLessThan` range check (Rsqrt, Div, MoS, trig) onto bucketed value range checks, with the bit-identical single-relation Rsqrt, (3) close the ScalarConstDiv gap through the same buckets, (4) importer-level GELU fusion, (5) re-measure dense-plus-LogUp and wider chunks on Akita before investing in either. Items 1–3 leave output bits unchanged.

### Cited Findings
- **[CODE]** The reusable building blocks exist: packed buckets with `CycleWeight::Packed` (`jolt-atlas-verifier/src/onnx_proof/global_clamp.rs:1-18`), value sumcheck + Booleanity linear term (`clamp_split.rs:1-56`, `atlas-sumcheck/src/booleanity/mod.rs` `LinearTerm`), deferred batches (`deferred_lookups.rs`).
- **[CODE]** Process requirements for protocol changes: a new relation needs its `Spec` binding and `S ⊆ R` note; a new proof field needs a `mutate::Site`; protocol-level changes need the zoo tests and one real-model run; an output change needs a new `SemVersion` and re-pinned golden hashes — `CLAUDE.md` ("Prover/Verifier Invariants"), `atlas-trace/src/semantics/mod.rs:1-16`.
- **[LIT]** Supporting precedents: single range relation for sqrt/div and fusion of adjacent rounding constraints — [zkGPT](https://home.cse.ust.hk/~kaichen/papers/zkgpt-security25.pdf); single-node GELU lookup — [ZKTorch](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07031v2); address width as the only per-lookup lever — [ePrint 2026/1390](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1390).

Soundness caveats to carry into any spec:
- A1 and R2 replace table lookups (whose range guarantee comes from the table) with algebraic identities over chunk-defined values; each needs an explicit no-wraparound bound in the 128-bit field. For R1 the largest term is `(out+1)²·x < 2^44·2^31 = 2^75`, comfortably below the modulus, but `out` and `x` must themselves be range-bound by something: `x` is an upstream node output and `out` is today bounded only through the lookup operand `2·out+1`, which R2 removes. R2 must therefore add an explicit range check on `out` (about 6 chunks at 22 bits) or rely on a downstream clamp.
- The `x ≤ 0 → 0` branch of `rsqrt` is rejected today rather than proven; R1 keeps that behaviour only if `x ≥ 1` is still enforced (`b ≥ 0` with `out ≥ 0` does not by itself exclude `x ≤ 0`).
- Bucketed checks tie many nodes to one set of chunks through random `γ_n` and per-node points; the existing argument (`clamp_split.rs:28-33`) depends on those challenges being drawn after the chunks are committed, which new members must preserve.

- Whether proposal 1's saving is material for GPT-2 specifically depends on how many activation elements it has relative to its clamp buckets, which was not counted.
- The Div quotient-range question and the remainder-bucket width question (both in the Div section) should be resolved before items 2b and 3 reuse those mechanisms.
