## 1. What changed in 2025-2026 about provable versus conjectured security, and what it does to query counts and proof sizes

### Takeaway
The "up-to-capacity" proximity-gap conjectures that FRI/STIR/WHIR deployments used for parameters were refuted in late 2025 and again in April 2026; the proven frontier is now the Johnson radius with much better error terms (BCHKS25) plus a first proven sliver beyond Johnson (Dao-Kominers-Thaler, Sept-Nov 2026). The practical price of "proven only" is roughly 1.8-1.9x proof size at equal rate (leanMultisig), i.e. about half the security bits per query, and the newest systems (FlashProve, Magnetar) both ship proven-regime parameters.

### Cited Findings
- Crites and Stewart (Web3 Foundation) disprove the BCIKS correlated-agreement up-to-capacity conjecture, WHIR's mutual-correlated-agreement up-to-capacity conjecture, and DEEP-FRI's list-decodability up-to-capacity conjecture, and propose minimally modified conjectures — [ePrint 2025/2046, late 2025, PP, snippet only](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2046).
- Krachun, Kazanin, Haböck: for RS codes over multiplicative subgroups of prime fields, at distance θ = 1 − ρ − η with η = Θ(1/log n) there is an affine line not θ-close to the code that still contains 2^Ω(1/η) θ-close points; gives a slightly stronger list-decoding lower bound — [ePrint 2026/782, received 2026-04-20, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/782).
- Ben-Sasson, Carmon, Haböck, Kopparty, Saraf, "On Proximity Gaps for Reed-Solomon Codes": up to the Johnson radius, proximity loss 0 with O(n) exceptional z (previously O(n²)); up to unique decoding, arbitrarily small loss with O(1) exceptions (previously O(n)); lower bounds showing Ω(n^1.99) exceptions are needed at or beyond Johnson for some codes; better proximity gaps beyond Johnson would imply better list decoding — [ePrint 2025/2055, received 2025-11-06, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2055). FlashProve's protocol description cites it as "STOC 2026" — [protocol.md, TR](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/FlashProve/main/docs/protocol.md).
- Arnon, Boneh, Fenzi, "Open Problems in List Decoding and Correlated Agreement": the companion paper of the Proximity Prize; July 2026 revision added "updated KKH comparison and concrete estimate of attacks" and "an MCA lower bound" — [ePrint 2026/680, received 2026-04-07, rev. 2026-07-06, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/680).
- Ethereum Foundation Proximity Prize: $1,000,000 for two grand challenges (largest δ* with ε_mca ≤ 2^-128 for RS codes of rate 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16; and the analogous list-size challenge); judges Boneh, Fenzi, Arnon; submissions close 2027-12-31 and must be accepted at a peer-reviewed venue; the page header shows "$0" and names no winners — [proximityprize.org, fetched 2026-11-09, BL](https://proximityprize.org/).
- better.codes (EF Formal Verification team with Yukon and zkSecurity, live 2026-08-20): an open challenge where solvers' AI agents raise a Lean-kernel-checked soundness lower bound, in bits, for the toy problem "koalaIRS12" (ABF26 Construction 6.9) formalised in ArkLib, toward a fixed 128-bit target; the post reports no current record — [EF blog, 2026-08-20, BL](https://blog.ethereum.org/2026/08/20/better-codes-challenge).
- Dao, Kominers, Thaler, "Reed-Solomon Codes Beyond Johnson": an explicit agreement threshold a₁(ρ) strictly below √ρ; at a₁(ρ)+η₁ list size O(n/η₁²) and MCA error O(n²/(q·η₁⁴)); improves the BCHKS error at gap η₀ above Johnson from O(n/(q·η₀⁵)) to O(n/(q·η₀³)) in every characteristic; beyond Johnson needs large characteristic; bounds formalised in ArkLib; measured proof-size savings at unchanged security: ProveKit passport proofs −79.4 KB (11.1%), ZisK compressed final proofs −13.2 KB (4.63%), LambdaVM CPU subproofs −59.8 KB (4.85%) — [ePrint 2026/2056, received 2026-09-16, rev. 2026-11-03, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2056).
- Manzur: list-decodability at radius p with list size L implies MCA slightly above 1 − √(1−p) (previous best was strictly below), and this barrier cannot be beaten from list-decodability alone; MCA error O(d·max{1,nγ}/|S|) for the univariate powers generator up to min(unique decoding, generalised Johnson) — [ePrint 2026/2617, received 2026-10-23, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2617).
- Other 2026 entries seen in search only: Jeronimo, hidden-derivative framework for list decoding at capacity and MCA — [ECCC TR26-169, PP, snippet](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/169/download); Gao, Yang, Xu, Kan, counterexamples to list-decodability give MCA lower bounds — [arXiv 2607.10572, PP, snippet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10572); Okamoto claims CA up to capacity, which is in tension with Crites-Stewart and Krachun et al. — [ePrint 2025/1712, PP, snippet](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1712).
- A July 2026 SoK states the capacity conjectures "including the mutual-correlated-agreement conjecture behind the newest schemes, were disproved over large fields in late 2025", that soundness up to Johnson is unaffected, and calls the repricing "modest" in practice — [ePrint 2026/1367, 2026-07-02, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1367).
- Measured cost of proven versus conjectured, same system, same rate (leanMultisig, WHIR over KoalaBear degree-5 extension, M4 Max): XMSS aggregation proof 338 KiB proven vs 176 KiB conjectured at rate 1/2, and 228 vs 126 KiB at rate 1/4; recursion (n=1) 278 vs 147 KiB at rate 1/2, 188 vs 100 KiB at rate 1/4; prover throughput nearly equal (1319 vs 1345 XMSS/s at rate 1/2) — [leanMultisig README (fork), 2026, BL](https://github.com/scaraven/leanMultisig).
- Rule of thumb stated in a reviewer note: under Johnson, security bits per query are about ½·log₂(1/ρ) versus log₂(1/ρ) under the capacity conjecture; a proven-only 128-bit post-quantum design reached 293 KB only by using rate 1/3840 in the last layer, a 370-bit challenge field (because Johnson list sizes of 2^41-2^43 enter the OOD/fold error) and 32 bits of grinding per round, "roughly 2x bytes and ~50x final-layer prover work" versus conjectured parameters; the BCIKS20 Johnson error term m⁷n²/(ρ^1.5·|F|) was too weak and the schedule only closes with the tighter DKT26 line-MCA bound — [near-proof-arena issue #3, 2026-10-21, BL](https://github.com/ilblackdragon/near-proof-arena/issues/3).
- FlashProve's shipped parameters (Table 2): Goldilocks p = 2^64 − 2^32 + 1, challenges from the cubic extension F[ϑ], ϑ³ = 2; RS domain |D| = 2^32; rate ρ = 0.1041; proximity parameter δ = 0.6640 ("beyond unique decoding, strictly below the Johnson radius"); at most 62-64 opened columns per table; one out-of-domain answer per row; 20/28/32 bits of proof of work (request / shared / offline and link rounds) — [FlashProve paper Table 2 and §1.2, Nov 2026, TR, partial](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/paper/paper.pdf).
- FlashProve's coding lemma (machine-checked, after BCHKS25 Theorem 4.6): MCA for two words under generator (1, z) strictly below Johnson with error J/|K|, J polynomial in a multiplicity parameter mult = max(⌈√ρ/(1−√ρ−δ)⌉, 3) and linear in |D|; every round error ≤ 2^-128 only with the proof of work counted, about 2^-96 to 2^-110 without; "with 27 [bits] the bound exceeds 2^-128" for the 64 positions; overall soundness about q/2^124 + 2^-101 — [FlashProve paper §5.4, Lemma 5.13, Prop. 5.14, Remark 5.15, TR, partial](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/paper/paper.pdf).
- Magnetar claims "120 bits of provable security" and that it avoids "conjectured Reed-Solomon proximity gaps", and says some prior hash-based zkVMs offer about 100 bits or depend on conjectured soundness — [ePrint 2026/2423, 2026-10-09, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2423).
- Ligero re-analysed in the list-decoding regime with MCA: lower query complexity "without changing the protocol", plus an out-of-domain variant enabling RS rates above 1/2 — [ePrint 2026/2681, received 2026-10-29, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2681).

### Inferences
- FlashProve's numbers are self-consistent with the Johnson rule: √0.1041 = 0.3226, Johnson radius 0.6774, chosen δ = 0.6640; each query gives −log₂(1 − 0.664) = 1.57 bits; 64 queries give 100.7 bits, plus 28 bits of grinding = 128.7. Under the (refuted) capacity conjecture the same rate would give about 3.26 bits per query (about 31 queries for the same 100.7 bits); under unique decoding about 0.86 bits per query (about 117 queries). So 2026's proven regime costs about 2x the queries of the old conjectured regime and about half those of unique decoding.
- The lever that remains in the proven regime is rate: halving proof size means squaring 1/ρ (FlashProve uses about 1/9.6; the near-proof-arena design 1/3840 in its last layer), paid for in encoding and hashing work. The other lever is grinding (20-32 bits is now routine).
- Large Johnson-regime list sizes push the challenge field up: FlashProve uses a 192-bit extension of Goldilocks; a 128-bit field such as jolt-atlas's Fp128 would need its own error budget worked out (the J/|K| term with |D| up to 2^32 leaves roughly 128 − 32 − log₂(poly(mult)/ρ^1.5) bits). This is a calculation, not a cited result.
- Beyond-Johnson provable results (DKT26) currently buy 5-11% of proof size, not a regime change.

## 2. Committing small values cheaply in prime fields; is there binary-tower-like packing for integer witnesses in 2026?

### Takeaway
Yes, as of September-October 2026 there are two preprints that claim it: BitZ (commit integer bits in a packed binary-field hash PCS, prove evaluation claims over any ring via a homomorphism, range checks "essentially for free") and Mikros (algebraic vector packing that makes linear-time extension-field protocols bitwidth-aware over "arbitrary small fields"). FlashProve takes the opposite route: no packing at all, just dense 64-bit Goldilocks elements holding 2-4 "digits" per weight, with one approximate range test per matrix.

### Cited Findings
- BitZ: a hash-based PCS for multilinear polynomials with coefficients in any finitely generated ring S (finite field, integers, cyclotomic ring); given a homomorphism ψ: S → R it proves evaluation claims over R for ψ(f); "cost depends almost entirely on the number of bits in the coefficients, not on S, R, or ψ"; range and bit-size checks "essentially for free"; integer coefficients are bit-decomposed and committed packed over F_{2^ν}; BitZ-SNARK uses Campanelli-Hall-Andersen fingerprinting (commit over Z, prove constraints over a random prime field F_q, ψ = reduction mod q); it also lets an integer-constraint SNARK and a binary-field SNARK such as Binius64 share one committed witness. Benchmarks: about 9 million proved 32-bit integer multiplications per second on a MacBook Air M5 (10 threads, CPU only) with proofs under 200 kB; SHA-256 of 2 kB plus P-256 ECDSA verification in 32 ms single-threaded, 3.1 ms verifier, 65 kB proof — [ePrint 2026/2141, received 2026-09-21, rev. 2026-10-04, World Foundation / Nethermind / Reilabs / Ivy, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2141).
- Mikros: "algebraic vector packing" packs length-N = mκ vectors over a base field F into length-m vectors over a degree-κ extension E, turning length-N relations over F into length-m relations over E; unifies bitwidth-aware inner-product, Hadamard-product and PCS; first bitwidth-aware grand-product and permutation argument; prover cost scales with witness bitwidth once wiring is amortised across instances; "does not depend on binary-field structure"; 2.19x / 2.55x / 1.85x faster than Flock on batches of 256 SHA-256 / Keccak / BLAKE3 — [ePrint 2026/2646, 2026-10-26, single author, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2646).
- Zinc+ (the predecessor line of BitZ): SNARKs over Q[X], Z[X] and F_q[X] at once, built from a hash-based IOPP for multilinear polynomials over Q[X] or F_q[X]; new "Integer Pseudo-Reed-Solomon" codes with FFT encoding and bounded norm growth; "can be instantiated end-to-end or as a lightweight extension to any existing hash-based SNARK over F_q"; 7 SHA-256 compressions plus ECDSA MSM in 40.6 ms prover, 7.0 ms verifier, 198 KB proof on a MacBook Air M4 — [ePrint 2026/855, 2026-05-01, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/855).
- LaBinius: a lattice PCS over a composite modulus with NTT-friendly prime factors where "the evaluation claim lives in a binary extension field, with no bitness checks or overhead over a binary arithmetisation"; commitment arithmetic and constraint arithmetic are decoupled; plugs into Binius and Flock front ends "almost black-box"; single-core proving at most 1.2x the time of each system's hash-based prover; over 67,000 BLAKE3 compressions per second, verification under 40 ms; with LaBRADOR as recursive compressor under 2x the hash-based provers and proofs below 100 KiB, about 5x smaller than Binius and Flock — [ePrint 2026/2103, 2026-09-18, Osadnik and Seiler, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2103).
- FlashProve's choice: weights are "written as a matrix of digits" (two digits per weight, four for embedding entries), encoded row by row over the 64-bit Goldilocks field with a masked Reed-Solomon code; no small-field packing is described — [FlashProve paper §1.2, §4.2, TR, partial](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/paper/paper.pdf).
- FlashProve's approximate range test: for each segment the verifier samples factors in {−1, 0, 1}; a digit lookup shows the combined trace values are small, which gives a looser bound on every entry and makes equations mod p hold over the integers ("no-overflow conditions") — [protocol.md §6.3, TR](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/FlashProve/main/docs/protocol.md). Parameters: the range-test matrix Θ has 128 columns and each entry of the product lies in [−2^17, 2^17), "sent as two digits"; soundness loss "the share 2^-128 of all matrices, 128 independent columns" — [FlashProve paper Table 2 and Table 10 row, TR, partial](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/paper/paper.pdf).
- Small prime fields in deployed hash-based multilinear systems in 2026: KoalaBear with degree-5 (leanMultisig) or degree-8 extension (Sable, WHIR EVM verifier "octic"); Goldilocks with cubic extension (FlashProve) — [leanMultisig, BL](https://github.com/scaraven/leanMultisig); [Sable README, 2026, BL](https://github.com/moonmath-ai/Sable); [FlashProve paper Table 2, TR](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/paper/paper.pdf).
- Jagged PCS (stacked multilinears): commit the whole trace as one polynomial and let the verifier emulate access to each column; no extra prover oracles; prover cost "dominated by 5 finite field multiplications per element in the trace area"; verifier circuit depends only on total trace area; now "a minor revision of an IACR publication in EUROCRYPT 2026" — [ePrint 2025/917, rev. 2026-04-13, PR, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/917).
- A July 2026 SoK on field choice identifies "the lack of a controlled cross-field comparison as the field's main empirical gap" — [ePrint 2026/1371, 2026-07-03, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1371).

### Inferences
- `docs/research-directions.md` §5 lists option C (bits in a binary hash commitment, arithmetic in the prime field) as "No known construction". BitZ's abstract claims exactly this object: binary-field packed commitment of integer bits, evaluation claims delivered in an arbitrary ring R through ψ. If the body confirms that R can be a fixed 128-bit prime field (not only a random fingerprinting prime), option C has a published construction with code. This is the single most relevant new result for jolt-atlas and it should be read in full before anything else in this note.
- LaBinius is the lattice-side analogue of the same decoupling (commitment in one ring, claims in another). It delivers claims in a binary extension field, which is the wrong target for integer matmul, so it does not by itself give option B; but it shows the "commitment arithmetic need not equal constraint arithmetic" idea works for lattices too, and its 1.2x-of-hash-based single-core figure is evidence against the blanket reading that lattice commitments are intrinsically the slow choice for bit-valued witnesses.
- Mikros's packing ratio κ is bounded by the extension degree used for soundness. Over KoalaBear with a degree-4 to 8 extension that is a 4-8x packing, far from the 128x of F_2^128; for b-bit integers in a 31-bit base field the gain over "one base-field element per value" comes from sumcheck/commit arithmetic staying in the base field, not from packing many values per element. (Reasoned from the abstract; not confirmed.)
- For a hash-based RS commitment the cost per committed element is dominated by NTT and hashing of field-element-sized symbols, so a 4-bit value in a 31-bit field costs about half of the same value in a 64-bit field and nothing less. That is why FlashProve wins by committing few, dense digits and making the range check nearly free, not by pay-per-bit.

### Gaps
- Whether BitZ's ψ can target a fixed large prime field and what the cost of the cross-ring evaluation argument is (per bit) is not on the abstract page.
- Whether Mikros's benchmarks include any prime-field instantiation (the abstract reports only comparisons with Flock, a binary-field system).
- I found no 2026 paper giving a ring-switching analogue specifically for prime fields beyond Mikros and Zinc+/BitZ, and no "MosaicPCS" source other than the Magnetar paper itself (the term returned no independent hits).
- No measurements of Mersenne-31 / circle-STARK multilinear commitments in 2026 were retrieved.

## 3. Binary-field line: integer arithmetic over binary fields and cross-field links

### Takeaway
2026 binary-field provers are an order of magnitude faster than 2025's on hash workloads and are being optimised weekly by AI agents on a public leaderboard, but integer multiplication in characteristic 2 is still done by an expensive detour (Binius64 multiplies "in the exponent" of F_2^128's multiplicative group). The new cross-field results are BitZ (binary commitment, arbitrary-ring claims) and LaBinius (lattice commitment, binary claims).

### Cited Findings
- BinarySpartan: Spartan over a binary field with Ligerito and Diamond-Posen ring switching; on an M4 Max (12 performance cores, no GPU, witness generation included) 872,000 BLAKE3 / 401,000 SHA-256 / 287,000 Keccak-f per second; 16.3-37.8x faster than Plonky3, 6.3-15.7x faster than Binius64, 4.0x faster than Hashcaster (Keccak); single SHA-256 of 2 KiB in 6.2 ms on the EF client-side proving benchmark — [ePrint 2026/1656, received 2026-08-11, rev. 2026-09-03, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1656).
- Magnetar (BinarySpartan as engine, RV64I): 7.2-8.7 million cycles/s for 2^22-cycle executions on 12 M4 Max performance cores, about 600 KiB proofs, 11-13 ms verifier, 120 bits provable; 24-66x SP1 Hypercube, 12-37x Jolt-Dory, 6-20x Jolt-Akita (geometric means 39x, 18x, 9.6x); an optimised version reaches 9.5-12.8 million cycles/s; Jolt proofs are "under 100 KiB" by comparison — [ePrint 2026/2423, 2026-10-09, rev. 2026-10-15, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2423).
- Flock (Bünz, Rothblum, Wang): hash-based SNARK for batched Boolean computation on binary fields with ring switching and R1CS; about 82k BLAKE3 / 42k SHA-256 / 30k Keccak per second on one M4 Max core, over 660k BLAKE3 on ten cores, under 250x native overhead; research prototype — [ePrint 2026/1329, received 2026-06-28, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1329); [Succinct blog, 2026, BL](https://blog.succinct.xyz/introducing-flock/).
- snark.fast (redirects to yukon.org/flock): a speed-up challenge for Flock measured as BLAKE3 compressions per second on a dedicated 16 vCPU / 32 GB x86 runner (262,144 compressions over the median of 100 trials). Record on 2026-11-09: 2,103,285 compressions/s (solver i34-9, credited model "Opus 5.5"), +425% over an implied baseline of about 400,600/s; proof 428.9 KiB; 203 promoted submissions from 28 solvers between 2026-08-14 and 2026-11-09; collaborators EF, Succinct, Espresso — [yukon.org/flock, fetched 2026-11-09, BL](https://www.yukon.org/flock).
- Binius64 Blueprint (2026-11-04): four native constraint forms over 64-bit words (vanishing, bitwise AND, 64-bit unsigned integer multiplication, GHASH-field multiplication); challenges from F_2^128 so prover arithmetic uses CLMUL/PMULL; ring switching to a packed witness that encodes 128 consecutive bits as one field element; zero knowledge by composing with a Spartan-based outer argument. IntMul "concerns an integer-arithmetic relation that has no direct expression in K; we instead encode it as a multiplicative-group identity in K* and reduce that identity via a tree of small-degree sumchecks": g^(a·b) = g^(2^64·c_hi)·g^(c_lo) with fixed-base and variable-base exponentiation subprotocols — [Binius64 Blueprint §abstract, §4.5, TR, partial](https://www.binius.xyz/spec.pdf).
- BitZ benchmarks integer multiplication directly: about 9 million 32-bit multiplications proved per second (M5 Air, 10 threads), and "SHA-256 followed by multiplication mod 2^32" as a mixed binary/integer statement over one commitment — [ePrint 2026/2141, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2141).
- LaBinius: see section 2; 5x smaller proofs than Binius and Flock, at most 1.2x their single-core prover time — [ePrint 2026/2103, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2103).
- Ligerito (the PCS under BinarySpartan/Magnetar): proof size about log(N)²/log log(N); works with any linear code with efficiently evaluable generator rows; 2^24 coefficients over a 32-bit binary field in 1.3 s on an M1 MacBook Pro (Julia), 255 KiB proof — [ePrint 2025/1187, 2025-06-24, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1187).

### Inferences
- Magnetar's 6-20x over Jolt-Akita is an end-to-end zkVM comparison in which arithmetisation (bit-valued R1CS, no one-hot lookups) and commitment both change. Combined with LaBinius's "lattice within 1.2x of hash-based on the same binary front end", the evidence says the dominant factor is what gets committed (packed bits versus one-hot digits over a 128-bit prime field), with hash-versus-lattice a second-order factor on CPU. This partly qualifies the premise that "our lattice commitment is the slow choice".
- Binius64's exponent trick confirms §3 of `research-directions.md`: integer multiplication in characteristic 2 is a dedicated sub-protocol per multiplication; nothing found in 2026 gives integer inner products (matmul) in binary fields at the O(mk + kn) cost of the prime-field sumcheck. BitZ avoids the problem by leaving characteristic 2 for the arithmetic.
- snark.fast shows a 5.25x speed-up of one prover in 12 weeks from agent-driven optimisation alone; published throughput figures for these systems age within weeks.

### Gaps
- Flock's and BinarySpartan's PCS parameters, proof sizes and verifier times are not on their abstract pages (snark.fast shows about 429 KiB for the Flock challenge workload).
- BitZ's per-multiplication constraint cost breakdown and whether inner products are supported natively are unknown without the PDF.
- Hashcaster and FRI-Binius were not revisited; only relative numbers above.

## 4. Concrete performance: throughput, proof size, verifier time (CPU and GPU), and leaderboards

### Takeaway
Published CPU numbers for hash-based multilinear/univariate PCS cluster at 25-35 million field elements per second on a 16-32 thread desktop for 32-64-bit fields (commit plus open, 2^24 elements in roughly 0.5-1.3 s), proofs of 60-600 KiB and verifiers of 0.4-30 ms at about 100 bits. The only GPU data point at scale is FlashProve: about 8.2 x 10^11 committed digits (twice, with masking) in 39 minutes on one H100.

### Cited Findings
- ReedWeave (interleaved RS plus FRI-style folding), Rust, Goldilocks, 32 threads, 100-bit, d = 2^24, ρ = 1/4, m = 64, DEEP variant: commit 475.0 ms, open 111.6 ms, verify 2.07 ms, proof 594.7 KiB; versus FRI: commit 6.9x faster, open 51.5x faster, verify 4.2x faster, proof 0.69x; versus STIR: commit 2.9x faster, open 68.5x faster, verify 2.1x faster, proof 2.31x larger — [ePrint 2026/2147, received 2026-09-22, rev. 2026-10-29, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2147).
- LiftWHIR (interleaving plus DEEP, both sub-tasks proven with WHIR), n = 2^20 over a 255-bit prime field, rate 1/2 / 1/4: evaluation phase 167 / 169 ms (4.4x / 6.7x faster than WHIR), total prover including commitment 808 / 1,474 ms (1.61x / 1.55x faster than WHIR), verifier 0.76 / 0.51 ms (WHIR 0.55 / 0.40 ms), proof 1.46x / 1.33x WHIR's — [ePrint 2026/1561, received 2026-07-30, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1561).
- WHIR baseline: PCS for degree 2^22 at 100 bits: 1.2 s commit and open, 63 KiB, 360 µs verification — [ePrint 2024/1586, EUROCRYPT 2025 per the task brief, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1586).
- WHIR in 2026 deployments: opening a 2^22 polynomial at 100 bits of provable (Johnson) soundness over KoalaBear with a quintic extension: 54,436 bytes of calldata, 3.6-5.6M gas; octic extension 47,780 bytes — [ethresear.ch / sol-whir-p3 PR #9, 2026, BL, snippet](https://ethresear.ch/t/evm-verification-of-whir-over-a-31-bit-field/24902). At 2^18, 80 bits, "CapacityBound", starting rate 2^-6, 30 PoW bits: Spartan-WHIR over KoalaBear 11,368 bytes of WHIR proof plus 2,627 bytes of PIOP — [client-side proving HackMD, 2026, BL](https://hackmd.io/@clientsideproving/pq-snark-verifier).
- Ligerito Rust crate (binary 32-bit elements, Ryzen 9 7945HX, 32 threads, AVX-512): 2^20 in 41 ms prove / 5 ms verify / 149 KB; 2^24 in 570 ms / 29 ms / 245 KB (29.4M elements/s; 4,339 ms on one thread); 2^28 in about 9 s / about 400 ms / about 2.4 MB; no GPU support ("expected 5-10x" listed as future work); security level not stated; the 0.6.2 release is marked yanked — [docs.rs ligerito 0.6.2 README, BL](https://docs.rs/crate/ligerito/0.6.2/source/README.md).
- LigeSIS (Ligero-style PCS with a homomorphic subset-sum hash over Goldilocks instead of Merkle hashing, for distributed provers): single-node performance "comparable" to WHIR; about 24x faster than distributed multilinear KZG — [ePrint 2026/751, 2026-04-16, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/751).
- leanMultisig (WHIR over KoalaBear, M4 Max CPU): 961-1,345 XMSS signatures aggregated per second; proofs 126-338 KiB depending on rate and regime — [leanMultisig README, BL](https://github.com/scaraven/leanMultisig).
- FlashProve, one H100 plus 24 CPU cores: committing "the first table and one copy" takes 2,323.77 s (n = 3); the table has 2^11 rows, each a message of ℓ = 402,653,184 entries encoded on a domain of 2^32 positions; proof 18.74-18.81 MB; C++ verifier 21.14 s on one thread, 3.53 GB; steady 111.23 tokens/s at batch 24; 269.7 GB host memory serving, 299.6 GB committing; hash is BLAKE2b — [FlashProve paper Table 2, Table 3; guide/performance.md; guide/how-it-works.md, TR](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/paper/paper.pdf).
- inferenceproofs.ai (release v0.1; 2 of 19 tracked systems benchmarked; jolt-atlas is listed as tracked, not benchmarked): FlashProve 68.21 µs proving time, 222.9 bytes of proof and 198.7 µs of checking (8 CPU threads) per token per Gbit of active weights, 61.28x serving overhead; SASH (Llama 3.1 8B) 75.41 ms, 639.9 kB, 51.04 ms and 15,760x — [inferenceproofs.ai, fetched 2026-11-09, BL](https://inferenceproofs.ai).
- Sable (MoonMath, 2026; zkML peer): KoalaBear, sumcheck plus LogUp-GKR, rate-1/2 RS, BLAKE3 Merkle, BaseFold opening over F_p^8; one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell (96 GB); Qwen2.5-32B, 16 generated tokens after a 4,096-token prompt: 92.5 s, 135 MiB proof; proofs 106-477 MiB overall; verifier 19-56 s on one core; 41-50x its own serving engine; 2^-103 per round with a 30-bit proof of work; not zero-knowledge — [Sable README, 2026, BL](https://github.com/moonmath-ai/Sable).
- Tessera (zkML attempt, issue dated 2026-11-09): measured H100 microkernel unit costs of 134.7 ps for encode-and-hash, 84.6 ps for a product-tree step and 297.0 ps for a fraction-tree (LogUp) step; the project's own rule says "stop and redesign" because these are 4-7x above its assumptions; no real model proven — [tessera issue #722, 2026-11-09, BL](https://github.com/AbdelStark/tessera/issues/722).
- GPU sumcheck: Chimera (hybrid CPU-GPU sumcheck backend for Spartan over BN254) gives 7.99x on the sumcheck region at N = 2^17, only 1.27-1.64x on complete proving time for N = 2^24-2^27, and a standalone quadratic sumcheck at 2^22 in 28.7 ms versus 84.2 ms for ICICLE — [ePrint 2026/1388, received 2026-07-07, rev. 2026-10-16, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1388). zkDuel characterises sumcheck kernels on an H100 across CUDA and Triton (CUDA's median advantage falls from 3.5x at 32-bit fields to 1.4x at 256-bit) — [arXiv 2610.37710, Oct 2026, PP, snippet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37710).
- GPU end-to-end caution: a profiling campaign on SP1 found "GPU kernels are only ~1/3 of proof wall" on an RTX 5090, the rest being serialization and host work — [hemilabs/sp1 PR #5, 2026, BL, snippet](https://github.com/hemilabs/sp1/pull/5).
- Leaderboards: snark.fast measures Flock BLAKE3 compressions per second (record 2,103,285/s, section 3) — [yukon.org/flock, BL](https://www.yukon.org/flock); better.codes measures machine-checked soundness bits for koalaIRS12 (section 1) — [EF blog, BL](https://blog.ethereum.org/2026/08/20/better-codes-challenge); zk.golf is named by the EF post as a related challenge alongside ecdsa.fail — [EF blog, BL](https://blog.ethereum.org/2026/08/20/better-codes-challenge).

### Inferences
- FlashProve per-element commit cost (my arithmetic, assuming the first table and the copy are the same shape and that the 2,324 s is all encoding and hashing, which overstates it because the offline and link proofs are included): message size 2^11 x 402,653,184 = 8.25 x 10^11 digits per table; two tables in 2,324 s gives about 7.1 x 10^8 committed message elements per second, about 1.4 ns per committed 64-bit element; in codeword terms 2 x 2^43 = 1.76 x 10^13 elements, about 0.13 ns per codeword element. That is within rounding of Tessera's independently measured 134.7 ps H100 "encode-and-hash" unit cost, if Tessera's unit is one codeword element (its issue does not define the unit).
- CPU per-element costs from the abstracts: ReedWeave commits 2^24 Goldilocks elements at rate 1/4 in 475 ms on 32 threads, about 28 ns per message element (35M elements/s); the same abstract implies STIR's commit at about 1.4 s and FRI's at about 3.3 s for that instance. The Ligerito crate proves (commit plus open) at 34 ns per 32-bit element on 32 threads and 259 ns on one thread. So a 2^28-element column costs roughly 8-10 s on a 16-32 thread x86 desktop today, before any agent-driven tuning of the kind snark.fast demonstrates. An M3 laptop with fewer cores would be slower; no M3 number exists.
- The GPU-to-CPU ratio for RS-encode-and-hash is therefore about 20x (1.4 ns versus about 28 ns per message element), with FlashProve at a lower rate (about 1/9.6 versus 1/4) and with zero-knowledge masking on. Per codeword element the gap is closer to 50x.
- FlashProve's 18.8 MB proof is not a property of WHIR-style tests in general: 62-64 opened columns of 2^11 Goldilocks elements is about 1 MB per table opening; the remainder must come from the 25 per-segment trace commitments and their proximity tests (inferred from Table 2; the paper's size breakdown was not located). The authors' open-problem list (§8.2) is about proofs of security properties; I found no stated proof-size optimisation roadmap in the sections read.
- For zkML specifically, the two GPU systems that commit the whole trace with RS plus Merkle (FlashProve, Sable) both land at 40-90x serving overhead and 20-480 MB proofs with 20-60 s verification. jolt-atlas's 2.4 MiB / 2 s is one to two orders of magnitude smaller and faster to verify, at a far lower throughput.

### Gaps
- No published GPU throughput for WHIR, Ligerito or BaseFold as standalone PCS libraries was found; the Ligerito crate explicitly has none.
- No 2026 numbers for Blaze, DeepFold, Brakedown-family or BaseFold as standalone PCS were retrieved; ReedWeave's abstract compares only with FRI and STIR (a search snippet mentions a WHIR row of about 232 KiB in its tables, unverified).
- Hardware for the WHIR paper's 1.2 s figure is not on the abstract page.
- zk.golf: could not determine what it measures or its records (page returned only a title; a search found nothing).
- FlashProve does not publish a time breakdown (NTT versus hashing versus sumcheck) or the number of committed trace elements per token in the files read.
- No like-for-like measurement of a hash-based commit against Akita on the same vector exists; LaBinius's 1.2x is for binary witnesses and its own lattice scheme, not Akita over Fp128.

## 5. Proof-size reduction for hash-based proofs; smallest proof and fastest verifier for 2^28-2^32 elements

### Takeaway
The cheap levers in 2026 are lower rate, grinding, Johnson-regime (and now slightly beyond-Johnson) analysis, and a final recursive compression; the strongest reported result is a lattice wrap: LaBinius plus LaBRADOR brings binary-field hash-style proofs under 100 KiB at under 2x prover cost. Pure hash-based proofs at 100-128 bits sit at about 50-65 KiB (2^22, WHIR) to 100-340 KiB (recursive WHIR aggregation), and I found no published hash-based proof below about 50 KiB for statements of 2^28 or more.

### Cited Findings
- LaBinius with LaBRADOR as recursive compressor: proving under 2x the hash-based provers at the largest sizes, proofs below 100 KiB, about 5x smaller than Binius and Flock; described by its authors as the smallest quantum-safe proof of a standard hash to date (per search summary) — [ePrint 2026/2103, 2026-09-18, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2103).
- Recursion with WHIR over KoalaBear (leanMultisig): n-to-1 recursion proofs of 100-108 KiB (rate 1/4, conjectured) up to 278-312 KiB (rate 1/2, proven), 0.2-0.4 s per recursive proof on an M4 Max; the README notes 128-bit security needs larger digests and is a TODO — [leanMultisig README, BL](https://github.com/scaraven/leanMultisig).
- WHIR at 2^22: 63 KiB and 360 µs verification at 100 bits (2024 paper, conjectured-regime headline) — [ePrint 2024/1586, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1586); 54.4 kB of calldata at 100 bits provable (Johnson) over KoalaBear quintic — [ethresear.ch, 2026, BL, snippet](https://ethresear.ch/t/evm-verification-of-whir-over-a-31-bit-field/24902).
- Low-rate final layer: a proven-only 128-bit post-quantum tail of 293 KB uses rate 1/3840, about 127-132 queries over four rounds, a 370-bit field, 32-bit grinding; the reviewer estimates about 150 KB without the large-field costs and "~50x final-layer prover work" versus conjectured parameters — [near-proof-arena issue #3, 2026-10-21, BL](https://github.com/ilblackdragon/near-proof-arena/issues/3).
- Beyond-Johnson analysis: 4.6-11.1% smaller proofs in three production systems at unchanged security — [ePrint 2026/2056, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2056).
- Magnetar: about 600 KiB and 11-13 ms verification for 2^22 RV64 cycles at 120 bits provable; 4.6x smaller than SP1 Hypercube; verification "mostly hash evaluation", which the author gives as the reason recursion is efficient — [ePrint 2026/2423, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2423).
- Accumulation for RS codes: Arc, "Accumulation for Reed-Solomon Codes" (DBLP lists it in CRYPTO 2025 (7), pages 128-160; the author list in the search summary was inconsistent and was not verified against the paper) — [ePrint 2024/1731, PR, snippet](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1731); "Linear-Time Accumulation Schemes" (Bünz, Chiesa, Fenzi, Wang; WARP): first accumulation scheme with linear prover time and logarithmic verifier, hash-based in the ROM, unbounded depth; TCC 2025 — [ePrint 2025/753, rev. 2025-11-15, PR, snippet](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/753).
- Lattice PCS proof sizes for comparison: Grand Danois (on Hachi) estimates roughly 80 KB for 2^32-size evaluations under vSIS — [ePrint 2026/1196, PP, search snippet](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1196); a16z's Aerie reports a 66 KiB lattice proof covering 32,768 signatures — [a16z crypto, 2026, BL, snippet](https://a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/fast-post-quantum-signature-aggregation-with-lattice-snarks); a 2025 estimate puts the most succinct post-quantum SNARKs (WHIR, LaBRADOR) at about 50-100 KB — [ePrint 2025/1905, PP, snippet](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1905).
- Jagged PCS is motivated by recursion: its verifier is an arithmetic circuit depending only on total trace area — [ePrint 2025/917, EUROCRYPT 2026, PR, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/917).
- FlashProve's related-work section notes ZK-DeepSeek "composes proofs recursively into one of constant size, without a trusted setup" for a 671B-parameter integer model (not read by the FlashProve authors beyond that, and not by me) — [FlashProve paper §9, TR](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/paper/paper.pdf).

### Inferences
- Smallest/fastest for a 2^28-2^32 statement, by family, on the evidence retrieved: (a) lattice or lattice-wrapped: about 80-100 KiB (Grand Danois estimate at 2^32; LaBinius plus LaBRADOR), tens of ms to verify; (b) pure hash-based with one recursion layer: about 100-340 KiB, sub-second recursion step, ms-scale verification; (c) pure hash-based single shot: several hundred KiB to MBs (Magnetar 600 KiB at 2^22 cycles; Ligerito crate about 2.4 MB at 2^28). WHIR-style polylog growth suggests a direct 2^30 opening in the proven regime at rate 1/4 would be a few hundred KiB, but I found no published figure and this is an extrapolation.
- A pairing wrap (Groth16, about 200 bytes) is the smallest option but gives up post-quantum security, which every 2026 system surveyed here treats as a requirement.
- jolt-atlas's current 2.4 MiB is already smaller than every zkML hash-based system and larger than what the lattice family now claims at 2^32; the hybrid that the literature now points to is "fast commitment for the bulk, lattice compressor for the tail", which is what LaBinius reports and what jolt-atlas could do with Akita in the compressor role.

### Gaps
- No source gives WHIR, Ligerito or BaseFold proof size and verifier time at 2^28-2^32 with 128-bit proven security.
- Arc and WARP were not read; their concrete costs (accumulator size, decider time) and whether any 2026 system deploys them are unknown. FlashProve's works list does not include either in the portion read.
- LaBRADOR-wrap costs for a prime-field (non-binary) hash-based proof are not reported anywhere retrieved.

## 6. Zero-knowledge for hash-based IOPs at low overhead; commit-and-prove reuse

### Takeaway
Two 2026 results make ZK nearly free for hash-based multilinear systems: Chiesa-Fenzi-Weissenberg's ZK IOPP for constrained interleaved codes ("negligible overhead", the basis of FlashProve's proximity test) and VEIL (3% prover, 22% verifier, 12% proof overhead at 2^29 elements). Reusing one commitment across many proofs is solved in FlashProve by a query budget plus re-randomised copies tied together with link proofs.

### Cited Findings
- CFW26: an IOPP for constrained interleaved linear codes with honest-verifier ZK at "negligible overhead compared to the (non-zero-knowledge) state of the art", round-by-round knowledge soundness with a straightline extractor; built from a ZK sumcheck reduction and a ZK code-switching reduction; highlights high-distance codes from dispersers — [ePrint 2026/391, 2026-02-25, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/391). FlashProve states its `blocks/` proximity test is this test "with differences listed in PUBLISHED-WORKS.md" — [FlashProve how-it-works and PUBLISHED-WORKS.md, TR](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/FlashProve/main/PUBLISHED-WORKS.md).
- VEIL (Succinct): a compiler that separates a hash-based multilinear proof system's algebraic interactions from its hashing and applies a ZK wrapper only to the algebraic part; proof of concept over a 31-bit prime field on a trace of 2^29 elements: about 3% prover, 22% verifier and 12% proof-size overhead — [ePrint 2026/683, 2026-04-07, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/683). FlashProve cites VEIL for "masks under committed pads" — [PUBLISHED-WORKS.md, TR](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/FlashProve/main/PUBLISHED-WORKS.md).
- Masked encoding in FlashProve: each row's message gets t = 44,040,191 random low coefficients against ℓ = 402,653,184 message entries; "any t columns of this committed table are independent of the weights; t is the opening budget"; one copy serves at most 688,127 batches (ν_copy + N·ν_on ≤ t with 64 columns per batch) and at most 710,324 copies can be linked to the first table — [FlashProve paper Table 2, §1.2, TR, partial](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/paper/paper.pdf).
- Link proof: the first table's and a copy's rows are folded with the same random factors, both folds are committed, random columns are opened and a proximity test run; the folds must agree at a random point in the weight part — [protocol.md §6.2, TR](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/FlashProve/main/docs/protocol.md).
- Reported ZK guarantees: zero knowledge error below 2^-154 for up to 688,127 replies and 2^64 hash queries, one bit per request revealed; honest-verifier ZK ≤ 2^-135; proved in Lean for one request per proof at exact match, "nothing for the programs" — [FlashProve paper §1.3, TR, partial](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/paper/paper.pdf).
- Cost model for commit-and-prove reuse: "the table is committed once, and a batch opens one copy once for all its requests, in at most ν_on columns, as in Brakedown and TensorSwitch" — [FlashProve paper §1.2, TR](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/paper/paper.pdf).
- Ligero variant with out-of-domain sampling achieves perfect honest-verifier ZK with rates above 1/2 — [ePrint 2026/2681, 2026-10-29, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2681).
- Binius64 obtains ZK by composing its core protocol with a Spartan-based outer argument — [Binius64 Blueprint abstract, 2026-11-04, TR](https://www.binius.xyz/spec.pdf).
- Celer (CRYPTO 2026): a lookup argument whose number of commitments is sublinear in the query size m; at m = 2^28, table 2^16 ("a real case from proving Llama language model inference") 25.9x faster than plookup and 9.4x faster than LogUp; FlashProve cites it for "lookup with no further commitments" — [ePrint 2026/1453, 2026-07-16, PR, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1453).

### Inferences
- FlashProve's ZK mask overhead on the committed weights is t/ℓ = 10.9% extra message length; this is the price of a 44-million-column opening budget, not of ZK per se. A system that opens a commitment once (per-request trace commitments) needs only about the number of queries (tens to hundreds of elements) of masking per row.
- For jolt-atlas, committed-weights mode (listed in research-directions §8 as a direction) has a concrete template: commit once offline with a masked RS code, budget the openings, re-randomise with link proofs. Under a lattice PCS the analogous reuse is simpler (hiding commitments do not have a per-query budget), which is a point in the lattice column not mentioned in the existing document.

### Gaps
- CFW26's concrete overhead numbers and query complexity are not on its abstract page.
- VEIL's applicability to a WHIR-style PCS in the Johnson regime with OOD samples is not stated in the abstract.
- "Small-mask ZK sumcheck": beyond CFW26's ZK sumcheck reduction and the Libra / Chiesa-Forbes-Spooner references in FlashProve's works list, no separate 2026 paper was found.

## 7. GPU implementations and what they report

### Takeaway
There is very little published, PCS-level GPU data. The usable numbers are FlashProve (end-to-end, one H100), Sable (end-to-end, one RTX PRO 6000), Tessera's H100 microkernel unit costs, and two sumcheck-kernel studies; all agree that encode-and-hash runs at order 10^10 codeword elements per second on a datacentre GPU and that end-to-end gains are bounded by host-side work.

### Cited Findings
- FlashProve requires x86-64 with SHA extensions, one H100 80 GB, CUDA 13.2; weights (232 GB) stay in host memory and are streamed; the run is proved in 25 segments "so that one GPU's memory can suffice"; batches of 64 hit 80,629 of 81,559 MiB and "are not to be relied on" — [FlashProve README, paper §1.2 and §7.2, TR](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve).
- FlashProve cites Özerk et al. (ePrint 2021/124) for its GPU NTT and uses BLAKE2b for Merkle trees and Fiat-Shamir, with truncated Merkle top layers / Merkle caps (Aumasson-Endignoux; Chiesa-Yogev) — [PUBLISHED-WORKS.md; guide/how-it-works.md, TR](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/FlashProve/main/PUBLISHED-WORKS.md).
- Sable: CUDA/NVRTC kernels driven from Python (PyTorch, CuPy), BLAKE3 Merkle trees, rate-1/2 RS over KoalaBear, BaseFold; 18-82 GB of GPU memory and 72-175 GB of host memory for 30-32B-parameter models; about 6.4 cents per 4,096-token request on Qwen2.5-32B at public GPU prices — [Sable README, 2026, BL](https://github.com/moonmath-ai/Sable).
- Tessera H100 unit costs: 134.7 ps encode-and-hash, 84.6 ps product-tree, 297.0 ps fraction-tree, bit-exact against CPU — [tessera issue #722, 2026-11-09, BL](https://github.com/AbdelStark/tessera/issues/722).
- Chimera: keeping polynomial data resident on the GPU across sumcheck rounds and returning only transcript values gives 7.99x on the sumcheck region but 1.27-1.64x end to end (BN254 Spartan, N = 2^24-2^27) — [ePrint 2026/1388, PP, abs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1388).
- zkDuel: on an H100, CUDA's advantage over Triton for sumcheck kernels shrinks from 3.5x at 32-bit to 1.4x at 256-bit fields — [arXiv 2610.37710, Oct 2026, PP, snippet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37710).
- SP1 on an RTX 5090: GPU kernels are about one third of proof wall time; kernel-level optimisation had no end-to-end effect (479.4 s versus 479.6 s) — [hemilabs/sp1 PR #5, 2026, BL, snippet](https://github.com/hemilabs/sp1/pull/5).
- Ligerito's Rust crate has no GPU or WebGPU path — [docs.rs README, BL](https://docs.rs/crate/ligerito/0.6.2/source/README.md).

### Inferences
- LogUp-style fraction trees are the most expensive GPU kernel per element in Tessera's measurements (297 ps versus 135 ps for encode-and-hash), consistent with FlashProve's decision to replace per-value lookups with a per-matrix random-sum test and with Celer's focus on large-query lookups.
- Small fields are where GPUs help most (zkDuel's 3.5x at 32 bits versus 1.4x at 256 bits is about the programming model, but the direction matches the general observation that 31/64-bit arithmetic maps to GPU integer units). A 128-bit prime field is a poor fit for the GPU path regardless of PCS.
- An Apple-silicon (Metal) path is absent from every system found; BinarySpartan and Magnetar explicitly report CPU-only M4 Max numbers.

### Gaps
- No published GPU Merkle-hashing throughput comparison (BLAKE2 vs BLAKE3 vs SHA-256) from 2026 was found; LaBinius's authors note (per a search summary, unverified) that Flock's default BLAKE3 Merkle trees were slower than hardware-accelerated SHA-256 on their CPU.
- No GPU numbers for Stwo, ZisK or Airbender were retrieved in this round.
- FlashProve's kernel-level breakdown is not in the documents read.

## 8. Ranked list: what jolt-atlas could adopt

### Takeaway
Read BitZ first, then prototype FlashProve's per-matrix range test and a Johnson-regime RS commitment for dense small integers; keep Akita as the compressor rather than discarding it. The premise that the lattice commitment is the bottleneck is only half supported: the 2026 evidence says the bottleneck is committing one-hot digits over a 128-bit field, and both a hash-based and a lattice commitment get fast once the committed object is small and dense.

### Cited Findings
Each item cites the findings above; effects on jolt-atlas are estimates, not measurements.

1. **Per-matrix approximate range test (FlashProve Range test), in the current Akita stack.** Parameters now known: 128 random columns with entries in {−1, 0, 1}, combined values in [−2^17, 2^17) sent as two looked-up digits, soundness loss 2^-128 from matrix choice — [FlashProve paper Table 2, protocol.md §6.3, TR](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/paper/paper.pdf).
   - Commit time: removes the clamp/no-overflow digits (8 of 12 one-hot digits per einsum output in research-directions §7.3); if commitment is 25 s of 40 s on GPT-2 and those digits are two thirds of it, the saving is of order 10-15 s. Proof size and verifier: slightly smaller (fewer openings). Unknown: imperfect completeness (FlashProve reports completeness error ≤ 2^-40 on served requests); how the {−1,0,1} matrix is made succinctly evaluable by our verifier; exact rounding still needs its remainder digits.
2. **Read BitZ (ePrint 2026/2141) in full and decide option C.** It claims a binary-packed hash commitment of integer bits with evaluation claims in an arbitrary ring and free range checks, 9M 32-bit multiplications/s on a laptop, proofs under 200 kB — [ePrint 2026/2141, PP](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2141).
   - Commit time: potentially pay-per-bit (a 28-bit accumulator costs 28 bits, not 12 one-hot digits), which is the research-directions §4.1 target; plausibly an order of magnitude on the 25 s if the cross-ring argument is cheap. Proof size: under 200 kB reported for its own SNARK. Verifier: 3 ms reported on a small statement. Unknown: whether the target ring can be our fixed Fp128; cost of the cross-ring evaluation argument per opening; security regime and bits; maturity (ten revisions in two weeks).
3. **Dense small-integer columns under a Johnson-regime RS + Merkle commitment (FlashProve/WHIR style), for the bulk witness.** Published rates: about 28-35 ns per 64-bit element on 32 CPU threads (ReedWeave commit; Ligerito crate), about 1.4 ns per element on an H100 (FlashProve, inferred) — [ePrint 2026/2147](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2147); [FlashProve paper Table 3](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/paper/paper.pdf).
   - Commit time: a GPT-2 trace of, say, 2^26-2^28 dense elements would commit in about 2-10 s on a desktop CPU; whether that beats 25 s on an M3 depends on the element count after items 1 and 4, which is unmeasured. Proof size: grows from 2.4 MiB toward 0.2-0.6 MiB per commitment opening in the proven regime, or tens of MB if every segment has its own commitment as in FlashProve/Sable. Verifier: ms per opening, but hashing-dominated. Unknown: requires moving the sumcheck field from Fp128 to Goldilocks or KoalaBear with an extension (a prime field has no small subfield), or a BitZ-style cross-ring link; the Johnson-regime error budget for a 128-bit challenge field at |D| ≥ 2^30 has to be computed (FlashProve uses a 192-bit extension).
4. **Single-commitment layout (Jagged PCS / MosaicPCS).** Jagged is now peer-reviewed (EUROCRYPT 2026) and costs about 5 field multiplications per trace element with no extra oracles — [ePrint 2025/917, PR](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/917).
   - Commit time: neutral. Proof size and verifier: removes per-polynomial openings and most of the 6.7 s opening-reduction stage noted in research-directions §4.2. Unknown: interaction with Akita's batching; PCS-agnostic, so it survives a later PCS change.
5. **Akita (or LaBRADOR-style) as the tail compressor of a hash-based bulk commitment.** LaBinius reports under 100 KiB at under 2x prover cost with this shape — [ePrint 2026/2103, PP](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2103).
   - Commit time: bulk at hash-based speed. Proof size: back to roughly today's or below (about 100 KiB class). Verifier: tens of ms. Unknown: no published instance over a prime field or with WHIR as the inner test; needs the verifier of the hash-based part expressed as a statement the lattice scheme can prove.
6. **Adopt 2026 proven-regime parameter practice if any hash-based component is added:** Johnson radius with OOD sample, 20-32 bits of grinding per query round, MCA bound from BCHKS25 / DKT26, machine-checked where possible (ArkLib) — [ePrint 2025/2055](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2055); [ePrint 2026/2056](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2056).
   - Effect: about 2x fewer queries than unique decoding, about 1.8-1.9x larger proofs than the refuted conjectured regime (leanMultisig). Unknown: DKT26 is unrefereed and its abstract page mentions listed gaps in a revision note (per a search summary).
7. **Zero knowledge via CFW26 or VEIL** when a hash-based commitment is in place: 3% prover / 12% proof / 22% verifier overhead reported — [ePrint 2026/683](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/683); [ePrint 2026/391](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/391).
   - Unknown: neither applies to the lattice stack; lattice zk remains the in-house plan.
8. **Large-query lookup without extra commitments (Celer, CRYPTO 2026)** for the remaining exact lookups: 9.4x over LogUp at m = 2^28, n = 2^16 — [ePrint 2026/1453, PR](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1453).
   - Commit time: fewer committed lookup columns. Unknown: comparison against one-hot Shout is not in the abstract; Tessera's measurements suggest LogUp fraction trees are the costliest GPU kernel.
9. **GPU path** only after the field question is settled: evidence favours 31/64-bit fields on GPU, and end-to-end gains are capped by host work (1.3-1.6x in Chimera; kernels one third of wall time in SP1) — [ePrint 2026/1388](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1388); [hemilabs/sp1 PR #5](https://github.com/hemilabs/sp1/pull/5).

### Inferences
- Ordering rationale: item 1 needs no new commitment scheme and attacks the largest known cost; item 2 is a reading task that decides whether item 3 needs a field change; item 4 is independent and survives any outcome; items 5-9 depend on 2-3.
- If items 1 and 4 are done and the remaining committed volume is small, the case for replacing Akita weakens considerably: the surveyed hash-based zkML systems pay 20-480 MB proofs and 20-60 s verification for their throughput, and the lattice family is where sub-100 KiB proofs at 2^32 are being claimed.

### Gaps
- The number of field elements jolt-atlas would commit for GPT-2 under a dense-integer layout is not known, so every commit-time estimate above is conditional.
- No source measures Akita on dense bounded columns (research-directions §9 step 1 remains the deciding experiment on the lattice side).
- No published system combines a prime-field sumcheck prover for ML inference with a hash-based PCS on CPU at laptop scale; FlashProve and Sable are GPU-only and Magnetar/Flock are binary-field.
