## Sumcheck prover optimizations: what each saves and on which summand shapes

### Takeaway
The 2025–2026 line (2025/1117 → 2026/587) gives three tools: small-value/round-batched proving for the first few rounds, a split-eq saving of about N large multiplications, and a new many-multilinear product algorithm (1.7–2.2x on Shout's high-degree sumcheck, degrees up to 32). On a 2-limb field like Fp128 the small-value gains are structurally smaller than the BN254 numbers in the papers; the part that transfers best to jolt-atlas is the high-degree product algorithm and upstream Jolt's deferred-reduction and pattern-table kernels.

### Cited Findings
- 2025/1117 (Bagad, Dao, Domb, Thaler; June 2025) classifies multiplications as ss (small×small), sl (small×large), ll (large×large) with ss ≪ sl ≪ ll; with N-limb elements, ll Montgomery costs about 2N²+1 word multiplications and their sl routine about 2N, "2.5–3 times faster" than ll. — [ePrint 2025/1117 mirror](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63970f25aa7b42e284492d52/6851e118b388a7d5dbf83fda_2025-1117.pdf)
- Baseline linear-time prover for a product of d multilinears: round 1 is d(d−1)·2^(ℓ−1) ss + d·2^(ℓ−1) sl; from round 2 on everything is ll: d(d−1)·2^(ℓ−i) for evaluations plus d·2^(ℓ−i) for binding. — [ePrint 2025/1117 mirror](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63970f25aa7b42e284492d52/6851e118b388a7d5dbf83fda_2025-1117.pdf)
- Small-value prover (Algorithm 4, Toom-Cook-style evaluation on a (d+1)^ℓ0 grid, delaying binding for ℓ0 rounds): Theorem 4.1 cost is (d−1)·((d+1)/2)^ℓ0·N ss + d·N sl + (d²/2^(ℓ0+1))·N ll; speedup ≈ 2^ℓ0 ≈ κ^(1/log(d+1)) where κ = ll/ss cost ratio: κ^0.63 for d = 2, κ^0.5 for d = 3; with κ ≈ 30 (BN254) and d = 2, ℓ0 ≈ 3.72 and "about 4 times". It also saves a factor 2^ℓ0 in space. — [ePrint 2025/1117 mirror](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63970f25aa7b42e284492d52/6851e118b388a7d5dbf83fda_2025-1117.pdf)
- Eq-factor optimization (Algorithm 5, split eq on top of Gruen): for g = eq(r,x)·Π p_k, dominant cost d(d+1)/2·N ll, "reducing from Algorithm 1's cost in the same setting by roughly N ll mults". Combined with small values the speedup is "about the square root" of the small-value-only speedup because the eq factor turns ss into sl. — [ePrint 2025/1117 mirror](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63970f25aa7b42e284492d52/6851e118b388a7d5dbf83fda_2025-1117.pdf)
- Measured (2025/1117): 2–3x on Spartan's first sumcheck in Jolt, 20x or more when the baseline is memory-bound. — [ePrint 2025/1117 mirror](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63970f25aa7b42e284492d52/6851e118b388a7d5dbf83fda_2025-1117.pdf)
- 2025/1117's position on Gruen's univariate skip (ePrint 2024/108): it needs values in a small subfield of an extension field ("applicable only in small-characteristic fields"), raises proof size, and may require the commitment to support a high-degree first variable; the small-value prover works for small integers inside a large prime field. — [ePrint 2025/1117 mirror](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63970f25aa7b42e284492d52/6851e118b388a7d5dbf83fda_2025-1117.pdf)
- 2026/587 (Dao, DeStefano, Bagad, Domb, Thaler; received 2026-03-24) is the extended version with **three** techniques: a new algorithm for products of many multilinears with fewer field multiplications; the small-value prover (now with a streaming variant); and the eq optimization. Reported: over an order of magnitude runtime and memory reduction on Jolt's Spartan sub-protocol; **1.7x–2.2x on "a key high-degree sum-check sub-protocol in the Shout batch-evaluation argument"**; degrees up to 32 supported; about 4x memory reduction for streaming versus linear-space univariate skip. (From search snippets of the abstract and body, not the PDF.) — [ePrint 2026/587](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/587)
- Quang Dao's blog post (2026-09-01) summarises the same work: 64-bit integer arithmetic is "about 10–50x faster than full field multiplication"; round batching over w rounds evaluates the w-variate round polynomial on {0,1,2}^w ((d+1)^w evaluations versus w(d+1)), giving roughly 2–3x at w ≈ 3 in the small-value regime and cutting streaming passes from O(n) to O(log n) (about 5 instead of 30 at n = 30); combined, "over 10x speedup and 17x memory reduction on a key sub-protocol" in Jolt. It cites Baweja et al., "Time-Space Trade-Offs for Sumcheck" (TCC 2025) for round batching in the streaming setting. — [CMU CSD PhD blog](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~csd-phd-blog/2026/sumcheck-proving/)
- Univariate skip in production Jolt: used in SpartanOuter and SpartanProductVirtualization; a May 2026 write-up describes a since-fixed verifier omission (output claim not checked against the uni-skip polynomial at the challenge, fixed in a16z/jolt PR #1474). Implementation lesson: the claim handed to the remainder sumcheck must be recomputed by the verifier. — [zkSecurity, 2026-05-09](https://blog.zksecurity.xyz/posts/jolt-uniskip-bug/)
- Jolt now runs the Spartan outer uni-skip in integers: "integer Spartan outer uni-skip with unreduced i128 fmadds" (PR #1967, merged 2026-10-06); uni-skip product evaluations reuse stage-1 claims (PR #2225, 2026-11-02: stage-2 CPU cycles −6 to −10%, whole prover −0.5%). — [PR #1967](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/1967), [PR #2225](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2225)
- Magnetar uses univariate skip in both outer sumchecks with different skip parameters per relation (footnote 5), citing Gruen. — [Magnetar](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- Streaming / small space: "Proving CPU executions in small space" (Nair, Thaler, Zhu, ePrint 2025/611) streams trace contributions across rounds; Magnetar notes its analysis "estimates a runtime overhead below a factor of two" and that no complete streaming zkVM prover has been evaluated experimentally. — [Magnetar §1.3](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- Jolt streaming status (Nov 2026): three **open** PRs add opt-in streaming kernels (instruction-input, Spartan outer later rounds, Spartan product later rounds); the instruction-input one holds about 2.5 GiB instead of about 5 GiB of tables at 2^26 cycles and recomputes early rounds from trace rows until dense tables fit a 4 GiB cap. — [PR #2373](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2373), [PR #2374](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2374), [PR #2375](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2375)
- GPU sumcheck: Chimera (ePrint 2026/1388) reports 7.96x on Spartan's committed-sumcheck region at N = 2^17 on BN254 with a chunking path for state exceeding GPU memory; FlashProve runs about 2000 sumchecks per request on one H100 over a ~64-bit prime with challenges from an extension. — [ePrint 2026/1388](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1388), [FlashProve report](https://assets.claude.com/a492b1fea80e6dcfe4e908ed77bd44961f8c02c2.pdf), [FlashProve protocol.md](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/docs/protocol.md)
- Binary-field skip round: "Speeding up Sumcheck using Algebraic Geometry" (Dalal, Bünz, Hemo, Rothblum; ePrint 2026/2494, 2026-10-15) builds a binarity-preserving multiplication code; skip round 2.36x and whole zerocheck 1.65x faster at 2^32 constraints. Binary-field only. — [ePrint 2026/2494](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2494)
- "Sum-check protocol for approximate computations" (Bitan, DeStefano, Goldwasser, Ishai, Kalai, Thaler; ePrint 2025/2152, revised 2026-03-16, EUROCRYPT 2026): a sumcheck whose verifier checks each round up to a tunable error δ, soundness degrading with δ/Δ, most natural over the complex numbers; aimed at avoiding finite-field encoding of real-valued data. — [ePrint 2025/2152](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2152)
- Upstream Jolt's Fp128 kernel work in Oct–Nov 2026 is dominated by deferred reduction and small-alphabet tables rather than new protocols: deferred reduction of signed wide products (#2255), across eq-table sums (#2211), in prefix product sums (#2214); compact 32-byte accumulators for pure sums (#2191, stage-5 CPU −3 to −12%); "categorical tables in early sumcheck rounds" where values from small alphabets are multiplied via lookup tables (#2159); no arithmetic on constant trace padding (#2141, #2182). — [PR #2255](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2255), [PR #2211](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2211), [PR #2191](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2191), [PR #2159](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2159), [PR #2182](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2182)

### Inferences
- **Fp128 shrinks the small-value win.** By the papers' own model κ ≈ 2N²+1: about 33 for 4-limb BN254 but about 9 for a 2-limb field, and a Solinas reduction is cheaper than Montgomery. κ^0.63 then gives roughly 4x in theory for d = 2 without an eq factor and about its square root (≈2x) with one, before implementation losses (the paper itself measured less than its estimate). Expect well under 2x on the affected rounds of `eq · small · small` relations (Mul, Square, Cube, softmax products). This supports the roadmap's placement of group E last.
- **Where each applies in jolt-atlas:**
  - *Matmul fold and rounds*: fold is `field × i64` — the sl regime; deferred/unreduced accumulation (roadmap A2) is exactly what upstream landed in #2255/#2211. After the fold the contraction is degree 2 over large values: none of small-value, uni-skip or Toom applies (agrees with roadmap §9).
  - *Element-wise products of small values*: small-value prover with ℓ0 ≈ 2–3 rounds plus split-eq; uni-skip is the less attractive option in a prime field (2025/1117's own argument) though Jolt's integer uni-skip shows it works for a fixed small-degree relation.
  - *Booleanity*: one-hot K = 16 digits have a tiny alphabet; upstream's gains came from pattern histograms and cached squared differences (see lookup section), not from the generic small-value algorithm.
  - *High-degree ra-virtualization (degree 9 / 17)*: the 2026/587 product algorithm (1.7–2.2x claimed) and upstream's balanced product tree are the directly relevant items.
  - *Claim/opening reduction*: split-eq and deferred reduction only.
- The approximate-computation sumcheck (2025/2152) is not a drop-in for an exact fixed-point contract; it targets real-valued claims and would change the statement proved.

### Gaps
- 2026/587 full text not retrieved: the exact multiplication count of the many-multilinear product algorithm, whether it is Karatsuba/Toom-style, and its degree-dependent table are unknown to these notes. The 1.7–2.2x figure is for Jolt's field and commitment at the time of writing the paper, not Fp128 specifically.
- No source found measuring small-value sumcheck on a 128-bit Solinas field; the κ ≈ 9 estimate is mine.
- Blendy and HOBBIT (ePrint 2025/1214) were found by title only; not read.

## Lookup and memory-checking arguments

### Takeaway
Nothing in 2026 beats one-hot Shout for a prime-field prover on per-lookup cost; the movement is in (a) how the one-hot digits are chunked and proved (upstream Jolt went to K = 16 committed chunks on Akita with wider virtual selectors and pattern-based Booleanity kernels), (b) committing small values densely instead of as one-hot digits when no range proof is needed, and (c) avoiding LogUp/grand-product for soundness reasons. Magnetar's one-hot refinement is a characteristic-2 Frobenius trick with no prime-field analogue, but its fusion of the Hamming-weight check into the read check does transfer.

### Cited Findings
- **Magnetar's refinement (Oct 2026).** Over F₂ a row sum of one only proves odd Hamming weight, so BinaryTwist/Shout prove weight ≤ 1 with a collision polynomial Δ(j; r, s) = Ã(r,j)·Ã(s,j) − Σ_u A(u,j)·eq(r,u)·eq(s,u), checked by a sumcheck over cycles that leaves three evaluations of Ã. The refinement sets s_h = r_h² so that, for bit-valued A, Frobenius gives Ã(s,j) = Ã(r,j)²; with c_h = 1 + r_h + r_h², r′_h = r_h³/c_h and C(r) = Π c_h, eq(r,u)³ = C(r)·eq(r′,u), collapsing the check to Δ*(j; r) = Ã(r,j)³ + C(r)·Ã(r′,j), leaving two evaluations instead of three. — [Magnetar §4](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- **BinaryShout** batches every address-bit and value claim into one sumcheck against Φ(k) = α₀ + Σ α_t k_t + Σ γ_h Val_h(k); the α₀ term forces Σ_k ra(k,·) = 1, so "BinaryShout therefore needs no separate non-emptiness check". BinaryTwist's identities are homogeneous in the address so it keeps a separate row-sum sumcheck. Costs: O(log T + log K) rounds, O(d·log T + log K) field elements, knowledge error O(d(log T + log K))/|K|; 3d output evaluation claims for Shout. — [Magnetar §4, Lemma 2](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- Magnetar still commits d·K^(1/d) one-hot bits per cycle for addresses; these are cheap there because bits pack 128 per field element. It uses BinaryShout **only for bytecode**; instruction semantics are constraints plus bit routings, and it states that over binary fields this "is faster than proving the same instruction semantics as lookups into a structured table of size 2^128 or more". — [Magnetar §1.3, §4](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- **Soundness of logarithmic-derivative / grand-product arguments.** Magnetar: "a grand-product or grand-sum argument over ℓ elements has soundness error about ℓ/|F|, so over a 128-bit field even an instance with ℓ = 2^20 loses 20 bits", against about 20d/|F| (≈6 bits for d = 3) for a sumcheck; it attributes SP1 Hypercube's and RISC Zero's 96–100-bit targets over ~124-bit extension fields to this. — [Magnetar §1.3, §2.2](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- **LogUp\*** (Soukhanov, ePrint 2025/946; explained in a 2026-10-12 post): for indexed lookups into small tables the prover commits the index column I and a table-sized vector Y = pushforward of eq(r,·) along I, never the looked-up values; a LogUp-GKR fraction check ties Y to I and one degree-2 sumcheck checks ⟨T, Y⟩ = V(r). Conservative error (N+M)/|F|; works in characteristic 2 because weights are eq values, not integer multiplicities. — [Frolov, "Understanding LogUp\*"](https://www.sasha.place/blog/2026/10/understanding-logup-star), [ePrint 2025/946](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/946.pdf)
- FlashProve's lookups are the Papini–Haböck LogUp-GKR product identity (ePrint 2023/1284) into tables of at most 2^16 entries (SiLU cut off, softmax exponential rounded to zero beyond that); digit lookups in its range test use tables of 2^10 and 2^8 rows. — [FlashProve PUBLISHED-WORKS.md](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/PUBLISHED-WORKS.md), [FlashProve report §5.2](https://assets.claude.com/a492b1fea80e6dcfe4e908ed77bd44961f8c02c2.pdf), [format 11-1](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/docs/format/11-1-machinery.md)
- **Separation principle (Min Jun Jo, ePrint 2026/1390, July 2026; abstract only).** In a Shout-style argument "the per-lookup proving work is a function of the access pattern alone, never of the table values"; activation structure (low degree, parity, kernel form) "can cheapen only a secondary, once-per-proof table term". In a pre-LN transformer the LayerNorm gain 1/σ is the only data-dependent amplifier of per-layer error, so a σ-floor lets one fixed proving precision serve every depth and cost stays near-linear in layers. Measured on EZKL/halo2 and Jolt Atlas; the remaining dial, the committed address width, is turned into "a bit-exact, upstreamed reduction in prover time". — [ePrint 2026/1390](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1390), [IACR news](https://iacr.org/news/item/28963)
- **Twist and Shout parameters as implemented.** Committing d one-hot chunks of size K^(1/d) raises per-variable degree from 3 to 2+d; third-party summary (2026-06-30) notes Jolt's `Inc` is one-dimensional "unlike in the paper" and mentions a "Twist and Shout via logup\*" note for hash-based commitments. Magnetar's bibliography lists the paper as Setty, Thaler, Zhu, CRYPTO 2026. — [Wiese summary](https://georgwiese.github.io/crypto-summaries/Papers/Twist-and-Shout), [Magnetar refs](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- **Chunk width on Akita, measured upstream.** PR #1994 (2026-10-02) moved Akita to 4-bit committed chunks (K = 16) at every trace length. At a padded 2^29 trace, K = 256 → K = 16: PCS commit 153.90 s → 80.71 s, sumcheck prover 232.09 s → 278.31 s, other PCS 44.89 s → 35.31 s, full prover 434.35 s → 397.03 s (one run each, pre-rebase). Akita's own default later changed to 16 (LayerZero akita PR 258, per Jolt PR #2246). — [PR #1994](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/1994), [PR #2246](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2246)
- **Committed versus virtual chunk width are separate dials.** PR #2188 (2026-10-30) keeps 4-bit committed selectors but 16-bit virtual selectors (each a product of four committed ones, instead of eight at 32 bits): instruction RA-virtualization spans took 38–44% less time, whole prover −4.5 to −8.9% at ~2^27 cycles, at the price of more factors in read/RAF checking. — [PR #2188](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2188)
- **High-degree RA products.** PR #1966 (2026-10-05): RA product grids built "as a balanced tree" with finite-difference extension, four- and eight-factor specialisations; PR #1994 replaced "Toom interpolation's linear solve with forward-difference interpolation"; PR #2159 uses pair-product tables for the first RA-virtualization round. — [PR #1966](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/1966), [PR #1994](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/1994), [PR #2159](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2159)
- **Booleanity kernels (prover-only).** PR #1923 (2026-09-24) proves the first four RAM Hamming-Booleanity rounds from 16-cycle bit patterns via a 2^15-bin histogram, then resumes dense Gruen rounds on a 16x smaller table; PR #2257 caches symmetric squared differences (Booleanity message CPU −11 to −16% at 2^20 rows, end-to-end mixed); related: #1932, #2196, #2200, #2201, #2210. — [PR #1923](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/1923), [PR #2257](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2257)
- **Dense commitment replacing one-hot digits.** PR #2145 (2026-10-28): Akita committed RV64 increments as balanced digit/carry one-hot columns (17 columns at K = 16, with Booleanity, digit-zero reduction and opening); it now commits "the existing fused increment polynomial directly with the bounded-dense PCS" and removes those relations. — [PR #2145](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2145)
- Other 2026 lookup papers found by title/snippet only: "Sumcheck bit packing" (Frigo, shelat; ePrint 2026/2770, 2026-11-04) packs m witness bits into one GF(2^m) element while still verifying linear claims per bit; "Lookup Arguments over Rings" (2026/471); "Post-Quantum Sublinear Lookup Arguments from Polynomial Preprocessing" (2026/2581); "Rogue: Updatable Matrix Lookup Arguments" (2026/1890); "SoK: Lookup Table Arguments" (2025/1876). — [2026/2770](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2770.pdf), [2026/471](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/471.pdf), [2026/2581](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2581.pdf), [2026/1890](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1890.pdf), [2025/1876](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1876.pdf)

### Inferences
- **No prime-field analogue of the Frobenius refinement is needed or available.** It relies on x ↦ x² being a field automorphism fixing F₂. In a large-characteristic field, Booleanity (degree 3, one evaluation of ra) plus a row sum equal to 1 already gives weight exactly one, which is as cheap as Magnetar's refined check (degree 3, two evaluations). What does transfer is the **α₀ trick**: add a random constant to the table combination in the read-raf sumcheck so the same instance enforces Σ_k ra(k, r) = 1, removing a separate Hamming-weight instance for read-only lookups. Small saving, verifier-visible.
- **Roadmap D1 (8-bit digits) now has upstream evidence against it.** On Akita, upstream measured K = 256 as slower to commit than K = 16 by about 1.9x and moved to K = 16. jolt-atlas already uses K = 16; the D1 microbenchmark is unlikely to be positive and can be deprioritised unless Akita's K = 256 path changed since.
- **The separate virtual-width dial is new relative to the roadmap.** jolt-atlas' degree-9 and degree-17 ra-virtualizations correspond to 8 and 16 committed 4-bit digits multiplied in one product. Upstream found four factors per virtual selector the best point; splitting a 16-digit product into several 4-factor products (more read-raf factors, lower virtualization degree) is a geometry change worth measuring on whatever high-degree instances survive roadmap groups B and C.
- **Separation principle = the roadmap's cost model.** "Table contents do not matter; cost depends on address width only" (roadmap §1) is the same statement; it independently supports "Not pursuing" piecewise-polynomial activations and half-tables, and supports B2/C2/C4 (fewer address digits) as the only lever.
- **LogUp-family judgement.** LogUp\* is the right shape for "many lookups, table 2^8–2^16, address given as a small committed value": commits one dense small index column plus a table-sized vector and no one-hot digits. But its error (N+M)/|F| costs log₂N bits over Fp128 (2^24 lookups → ~104 bits), it adds GKR layers, and the dense index column still needs its range established. This sharpens research-directions §4.4: D4 is only interesting if the dense-column commit is much cheaper than one-hot digits *and* a sub-128-bit level is acceptable or the fraction check is run over an extension.
- **Dense bounded columns (PR #2145)** show Akita's dense path is production-usable for small values upstream; that is the commitment primitive research-directions §5 option B and roadmap D3 ask about. The PR removes a representation, not a range proof, so it does not answer whether the bound is extractable.

### Gaps
- CRYPTO 2026 version of Twist and Shout not retrieved; what changed from ePrint 2025/105 is unknown. Magnetar attributes the collision-polynomial check to "Twist and Shout [55]", which may be a characteristic-2 restatement rather than the paper's prime-field check.
- 2026/1390 full text not retrieved: the exact address widths, the ops affected, the measured times/models/hardware, and the repository and PR the reduction was upstreamed into are all unknown. A `gh` search did not locate the PR.
- No 2026 source was found that measures bit-addressed lookups (table read as a degree-b multilinear composition over committed address bits, research-directions §4.3) against one-hot addressing in a prime field.
- The LogUp-GKR "fix" for large-instance soundness loss: no dedicated 2026 paper found; the options in practice are an extension field for the challenge or accepting a lower level (as SP1 Hypercube and RISC Zero do per Magnetar).

## Range proofs: approximate (random subset sums) and exact

### Takeaway
FlashProve's range check is the Lyubashevsky–Nguyen–Seiler approximate range proof with ternary coefficients, applied row-wise: for an m×k matrix it range-checks τ = 128 random ±1/0 combinations per row (two digits each, by lookup) instead of k cells, so the saving is about k/(2τ) and exists only for wide rows; it proves a looser bound ("no wrap-around"), with exact ranges still coming from lookups. The verifier does not evaluate the random matrix succinctly; it samples all K·τ trits.

### Cited Findings
- Report wording: the simplified IOP checks every trace value "one lookup per value. This is expensive, and FlashProve instead checks random sums of the entries of a whole matrix at once"; cost is imperfect completeness ("an honest prover is sometimes rejected (with tiny probability)") and a zero-knowledge caveat (a rejected request might leak about the weights; retry with fresh randomness planned). — [FlashProve report §5.3](https://assets.claude.com/a492b1fea80e6dcfe4e908ed77bd44961f8c02c2.pdf)
- Protocol description: after a segment's trace is committed "the verifier samples factors, each -1, 0 or 1. A lookup of digits shows that the trace's values, combined with these factors, are small. Except with small probability over the factors, the values themselves are then small too, within a looser bound." It is "an approximate range proof (not the range proof of 6.1)"; its role is that equations holding modulo the prime also hold over the integers, under public "no-overflow conditions" checked by the verifier. — [FlashProve protocol.md §4, §6.3](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/docs/protocol.md)
- Construction (format §11.1, "smallness test"): sampled matrix Θ_μ[c][u] ∈ {−1,0,1} per column c and combination u < τ; comb(μ,i,u) = Σ_c Θ_μ[c][u]·cell(μ,i,c) for each cell row i; each combination is committed as two digits, val = ZZ_lo + 2^10·ZZ_hi − 2^17 (10-bit low digit, 8-bit high digit), and one lookup with two calls (tables of 2^10 and 2^8 rows) bounds the digits. The digit array `ZZ` has τ rows and 2·mz columns (mz = total cell rows). The test's equations are folded into the segment's batched claim with powers of a challenge χ; they "have no round of their own". — [FlashProve format 11-1](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/docs/format/11-1-machinery.md)
- Parameters: τ equals the security parameter minus proof-of-work bits; shipped parameter files use τ = 128 with zero grinding; the round's error is 2^−T for T combinations; allowed τ ∈ {32, 64, 96, 128, 160, 192, 256}; at most 8191 columns per matrix. The verifier samples K·T trits, K the number of columns of all matrices in the segment's test. — [FlashProve format 08-parameters](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/docs/format/08-parameters.md)
- What is concluded: each integer is "above −2^18 and below 2^18. The bound is twice the offset 2^17. The test does not state that a cell is a byte." Cells are committed packed three to a field element at radix 2^20. — [FlashProve format 11-1](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/docs/format/11-1-machinery.md)
- Origins, as documented by the authors: "the approximate range check of [LNS20] section 2.7, one of the approximate range proofs of lattice-based cryptography, which bound the entries of a committed vector through random combinations with coefficients 0, +1 and −1"; coefficient law "0 with probability 1/2, +1 and −1 with 1/4 each" from Lyubashevsky–Nguyen–Seiler (ePrint 2020/1448, §2.7, Lemma 2.5/A.1) and Gentry–Halevi–Lyubashevsky (ePrint 2021/1397, §3.4, Lemma 3.4). GHL §3 is the source for the two-step pattern: prove coarsely that nothing wraps, then prove the exact statement; GHL §3.5 gets exact bounds from four squares, whereas in FlashProve "the exact ranges come from lookups". — [FlashProve PUBLISHED-WORKS.md](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/PUBLISHED-WORKS.md)
- The authors' own caveats: the 2^−N count is per column fixed before Θ is sampled; a union bound over columns leaves N − log₂(columns) bits; the adaptation to packed cells and digit lookups is argued anew and "has not been compared with Lemma 3.4" of GHL; a test harness with a mismatched digit layout accepted a cell of 1,000 under bound 32. — [FlashProve format 11-1](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/docs/format/11-1-machinery.md)
- Exact range proof in FlashProve (weights, one-time): weights are cut into centred signed-byte digits (LaBRADOR-style, low + 256·high) and "lookups show that the digits are in fixed small ranges". Rounded quotients and square roots are "taken from the prover and shown through a range-checked remainder", following ZKML and zkGPT (USENIX Security 2025; "distances to both ends of the range are looked up"); roots are rounded down. — [FlashProve PUBLISHED-WORKS.md](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/PUBLISHED-WORKS.md), [protocol.md §6.1](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/docs/protocol.md)
- FlashProve's overall accounting as stated in its protocol page: 96.23 bits without proof of work and 126.21 bits as a whole, with 341 proximity rounds resting on the Reed–Solomon proximity-gaps theorem of Ben-Sasson et al. (ePrint 2025/2055). — [FlashProve protocol.md §6.4](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/docs/protocol.md)
- Upstream Jolt range-check items: "reuse range-check sums across zero-prefix phases" (PR #2224, prover-only, <1% whole prover); "fix: range-check direct RAM loads" (PR #2190). No new range-proof protocol landed. — [PR #2224](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2224), [PR #2190](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2190)

### Inferences
- **This resolves the open reading in research-directions §7.3**: rounding stays exact through lookups/range-checked remainders (zkGPT-style), and the random sums carry only no-overflow. It also corrects the phrase "one check covers a whole tensor": the unit is one row, and each row still costs 2τ = 256 looked-up digits.
- **Break-even is row width, not tensor size.** Per row: k values checked individually versus τ combinations × 2 digits. At τ = 128 the approximate check wins only when k ≫ 256 in digit count (FlashProve's rows go up to 8191). For jolt-atlas shapes: MLP activations (k = 3072/4096 padded) gain about 4096·8 digits → 128·⌈(b+log₂√k+margin)/4⌉ ≈ 128·10 digits per row, roughly 25x fewer clamp digits; hidden-width tensors (k = 768/1024) about 6x; attention score rows at 16 tokens (k = 16) lose. Flattening a whole tensor into one long row makes it 128 combinations per tensor, at the price of a verifier pass over n·τ trits.
- **It works on a virtual vector, with one condition.** The link Σ_u χ^u·comb(u) = Σ_c (Σ_u χ^u Θ[c][u])·x(c) is an inner product with a public vector: one degree-2 sumcheck ending in a claim x(r), which is exactly what a virtual polynomial can absorb, plus the verifier's own evaluation of the combined Θ's multilinear extension. The lemma needs x fixed before Θ is sampled; a virtual x qualifies if it is a deterministic field function of data committed earlier in the transcript (e.g. quotient = (accumulator − committed remainder)/divisor, inductively through the graph), so all hint commitments for the covered tensors must precede the Θ challenge. This ordering argument is mine and needs a written proof.
- **Succinct Θ is the unresolved part.** FlashProve pays O(K·τ) verifier work and gets away with it because projection is along one axis. The LNS proof isolates one coordinate with independent coefficients; a tensor-product or otherwise low-entropy Θ with a cheap multilinear extension breaks that independence and I found no published analysis that restores it. Since the jolt-atlas verifier is already linear in the weights, a hash-expanded Θ with O(n) verifier additions per tensor is the low-risk first version.
- **Slack and exact semantics.** The proven bound is 2× the acceptance window, and the window must sit several standard deviations (σ ≈ B·√(k/2) for honest bound B) above honest sums; total slack is about 2t·√(k/2), i.e. 2^9–2^10 at k = 4096 with t ≈ 10–12. A saturating clamp at 2^31 is then provably the identity only when the honest bound is ≤ 2^31/slack ≈ 2^21. So the approximate check composes naturally with roadmap D2 (declared narrow widths): prover declares B per bucket, the approximate check shows |x| < slack·B ≤ 2^31, hence no saturation and bit-exact outputs; tensors that actually saturate fall back to exact digits. Remainders (4 digits) stay exact, as research-directions §7.3 proposes.
- **Grinding trades directly against τ.** FlashProve's parameter `work smallness` shows the intended lever: w proof-of-work bits allow τ = 128 − w combinations.
- For range checks cheaper than digit decomposition and still exact, the only 2026 mechanisms found are commitment-level (bit-committed witnesses in characteristic 2; pay-per-bit lattice commitments per research-directions §5), not new IOPs.

### Gaps

The slack arithmetic above is my estimate, not a published parameter set; FlashProve's own completeness error value was not found (only "tiny probability").
- No published soundness analysis found for approximate range proofs with structured (tensor/low-entropy) projection vectors, or for projecting a virtual (uncommitted) vector inside a GKR-style prover.
- No 2025–2026 paper found giving an exact, lookup-free, sumcheck-native range proof over a large prime field that undercuts digit decomposition. zkRNN (ePrint 2026/073) and verifiable-FHE truncation gadgets were not read.

## Constraint systems versus lookups

### Takeaway
Magnetar's "constraints beat lookups" result is explicitly a binary-field result built on a bit-committed witness that costs one bit per bit; in a prime field the same bits would each need a Booleanity proof, so the conclusion does not transfer wholesale. The transferable pieces are structural: aligned-subcube segment claims (Virtual Spartan) and public sparse routing tensors (BinaryRouter), which correspond to roadmap B4.

### Cited Findings
- Dual SIMD R1CS: one relation over F₂ for bit constraints and one over F₂¹²⁸ for packed word operations, reading the same bit-valued witness; in F₂ a 64-bit conditional select costs 64 constraints, in F₂¹²⁸ one, while a bit product in F₂¹²⁸ is a full field multiplication. Agreement is forced because both relations' claims are checked against the same commitment or the same auxiliary argument. — [Magnetar §2.2](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- Virtual Spartan: lay out the witness so each segment is an aligned subcube; "the claim on W is then a fixed linear combination of one claim per segment, so the prover sends the per-segment values and the verifier checks the combination with a handful of field operations, with no additional sum-check argument"; virtual segments' claims are forwarded to the argument that owns them. — [Magnetar §2.2, §5.3](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- BinaryRouter: Y(o,j) = Σ_{u,h} X(u,j)·Q(h,j)·R(o,u,h) with X source bits, Q a one-hot routing selector from the bytecode row, R a public sparse tensor; one sumcheck over cycle, source and routing indices, no extra commitments, verifier evaluates R̃ itself, prover linear in trace length, error O(log T)/|K|. With "a few hundred routings" Q is stored as two shorter one-hot fields, raising degree by one. Shifts and sub-word stores are the two instances; register-amount shifts are expanded into static-routing virtual instructions. — [Magnetar §2.2, §5.4](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- Motivation for routing: shifts and sub-word stores are the operations "that neither R1CS nor lookups prove cheaply". — [Magnetar §1.3](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- Results: 7.2–8.7 M cycles/s at 2^22 cycles, 120 bits provable security, ~600 KiB proofs, 11–13 ms verification on an M4 Max; 6–20x Jolt-Akita (geomean 9.6x); the optimized variant 9.5–12.8 M cycles/s with 36–40% less memory. — [Magnetar §1.2](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- BinarySpartan (Setty, ePrint 2026/1656, Aug–Sep 2026): Spartan over binary fields with Ligerito and ring switching; about 872k BLAKE3, 401k SHA-256 and 287k Keccak-f per second on an M4 Max. — [ePrint 2026/1656](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1656)
- Upstream Jolt is itself adding binary fields: a binary-field backend, sum-check over binary fields and an F8 uni-skip domain merged in October 2026 (PRs #2032–#2040); PR #2040 states "Akita is gaining binary-field commitment and opening (the LaBinius techniques)". — [PR #2033](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2033), [PR #2040](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2040)
- Upstream Jolt meanwhile keeps reducing lookup rows per instruction rather than abandoning lookups (e.g. DIV 24 → 12 rows, word-arithmetic fusions, W-aware shift tables). — [PR #1751](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/1751), [PR #1750](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/1750), [PR #1753](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/1753)

### Inferences
- **zkML analogue of Virtual Spartan = ties.** Concat/Slice/Reshape on aligned power-of-two segments reduce to a fixed linear combination of per-segment claims with zero rounds; this is the same mechanism as roadmap B4 and is independent corroboration that it is the right design.
- **zkML analogue of BinaryRouter.** A static, misaligned structural op is Y(o) = Σ_u X(u)·R(o,u) with public sparse R; the verifier should evaluate R̃ in O(nnz) or closed form (shifted eq), never by materialising a 2^n selector table (the cost roadmap B4 identifies). Data-dependent gathers are R with a committed one-hot Q, which is Shout; nothing is gained over the existing lookup.
- **Requantization as routing needs bits.** A power-of-two rescale is a bit routing of the accumulator's bits (research-directions §4.1), but in Fp128 each committed bit needs Booleanity and costs a full one-hot-like nonzero; routing only wins once a pay-per-bit commitment exists. The upstream binary-field/LaBinius work is the first concrete sign that such a commitment may arrive inside the Akita stack; it bears on research-directions §5 option C (bits in a binary commitment, arithmetic in the prime field) and should be watched before investing in a cross-characteristic link of our own.
- Replacing per-op sumchecks with one CCS/R1CS over the trace does not help matmul (Thaler's sumcheck already commits nothing) and would force committing intermediates; the per-op relation architecture remains the right one for the linear algebra.

### Gaps
- Magnetar repository/release status at Microsoft not verified (a third-party reimplementation exists on GitHub; not reviewed).
- No source found on CCS-based or routing-based zkML provers in 2026 with measurements. What "LaBinius" is beyond the PR's one-line description was not retrieved.

## Claim reduction and batching, and soundness accounting

### Takeaway
The current best practice is one commitment and one opening with free claim lifting: MosaicPCS (aligned subcubes, append selector bits, one degree-2 batching sumcheck) over JaggedPCS (needs an adapter sumcheck per component). Upstream Jolt-Akita moved the same way in September–October 2026 (one native commitment group, same-point openings, multi-point openings for advice). For soundness, sumcheck costs log-many bits while LogUp/grand-product cost log₂(instance size) bits; Jolt-Akita is quoted at about 122 bits.

### Cited Findings
- MosaicPCS: components p_i of size 2^ℓi placed on disjoint aligned subcubes of one G; G̃(r ‖ s_i) = p̃_i(r), so "the verifier simply appends the public suffix s_i to the point"; then one batching sumcheck Σ_i α_i v_i = Σ_x (Σ_i α_i eq(r_i‖s_i, x))·G(x) of degree two with knowledge error O(ℓ)/|K|, then ring switching (binary-field specific) and a single PCS opening. "Proving throughput is unaffected, since the prover commits to essentially the same bits". — [Magnetar §2.2, §5.2](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- Versus JaggedPCS (Hemo, Jue, Rabinovich, Roh, Rothblum; ePrint 2025/917): stacked families with arbitrary heights, but "a claim on a sparse component must first be reduced, through an additional adapter sum-check argument, to a claim on the committed dense polynomial"; Mosaic's batching step is the standard one every multi-claim scheme performs, not that adapter. — [Magnetar Remark 1, fn. 4](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- Upstream Jolt-Akita: PR #2012 (2026-10-07) replaced the selector-packed trace polynomial with "one native commitment group containing the actual trace columns. Every column has log_T + log_K variables and opens at the same point"; PR #1829 uses multi-point openings for advice claims; PR #1798 batches dense advice with the packed trace; PR #1731 specialises prefix-packed commitments; PR #1842 removes redundant opening framing and rehashing. — [PR #2012](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2012), [PR #1829](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/1829), [PR #1798](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/1798), [PR #1731](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/1731)
- FlashProve's endgame: every claim becomes a linear claim on an encoded message; all are batched into one and carried through the folds of a single proximity test; per-request claims on the weight table are combined as g·M·e + μ = v so the table is opened once per batch. — [FlashProve protocol.md §5.4, §6.3](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/docs/protocol.md)
- Soundness in bits: sumcheck over ℓ = 2^20 elements, degree 3: error ≈ 20·3/|F|, about 6 bits lost; grand-product/grand-sum: ≈ ℓ/|F|, 20 bits lost at 2^20. "Jolt-Akita's Module-SIS commitment targets 128 bits of security, but its sum-check arguments over a 128-bit field leave the overall level at about 122 bits." — [Magnetar §2.2, §6](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf)
- Grinding as a soundness lever is used in both newer systems' accounting (FlashProve: 96.23 bits before proof of work, 126.21 after; jolt-atlas' transcript already grinds before each challenge per its CLAUDE.md). Upstream bumped Akita to NIST category 3 Module-SIS parameters (PR #2107, 2026-10-21). — [FlashProve protocol.md §6.4](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/docs/protocol.md), [PR #2107](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2107)

### Inferences
- **Applies to claim/opening reduction (6.7 s on GPT-2).** jolt-atlas already pads to powers of two, so the Mosaic layout is available: lift every opening claim by appending selector bits, run one degree-2 batching sumcheck over the packed domain, open once. Because the packed G is one-hot-sparse, the batching sumcheck's early rounds cost is proportional to nonzeros; the saving is the per-polynomial reduction instances and bookkeeping (research-directions §4.2). The ring-switching step is not needed in a prime field. Upstream's "one native group, same point" design is the Akita-specific variant and is the lower-risk template since it is already measured there.
- **Accounting rule of thumb for Fp128.** Each batched sumcheck stage costs about (rounds × max degree)/2^128 plus its batching term; with degree-17 instances and ~30 rounds, a stage loses ~9 bits, and stages add. The ~122-bit figure for Jolt-Akita is the right order for jolt-atlas; degree reductions from roadmap B/C raise it slightly. Any LogUp-style component over N = 2^24–2^28 items would become the floor at 100–104 bits unless run over an extension, which is the quantitative case against roadmap D4.

### Gaps
- JaggedPCS paper not read; its adapter cost is known here only through Magnetar's remark.
- No 2026 source found on accumulation/folding of openings specifically for Akita beyond the PR descriptions; the Akita paper was not read.
- A per-stage soundness budget for jolt-atlas itself was not computed (out of scope; no code read).

## Ranked list of techniques to adopt

### Takeaway
The largest available saving is still commitment volume, so the approximate no-overflow check on wide tensors ranks first; single-commitment claim lifting second; RA-virtualization geometry and product kernels third. Small-value sumcheck and uni-skip rank low on Fp128, and 8-bit digits and LogUp-GKR should be dropped or parked.

### Cited Findings
- Evidence for each rank is cited in the sections above; cost baselines (25.2 s commitment, 5.3 s IOP, 6.7 s opening reduction on GPT-2; 8 of 12 einsum-output digits being clamp digits) are from the two repository docs named in the assignment, not from external sources. — [FlashProve format 11-1](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/main/docs/format/11-1-machinery.md), [Magnetar](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/magnetar-1.pdf), [a16z/jolt PR #1994](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/1994), [PR #2188](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/2188)

### Inferences
Ranked; "saving" figures are my estimates unless a measured source is named.

1. **Approximate no-overflow check (LNS ternary projections) for clamp digits on wide tensors, combined with declared widths (roadmap D2).**
   - Applies to: range checks (the 8 clamp digits per einsum/Add/Sub/Sum output); remainders stay exact.
   - Saving: per row of width k, 8k digits → about 128 × 9–10 digits; ~25x fewer clamp digits at k = 4096, ~6x at k = 1024, none below k ≈ 256 unless tensors are flattened. If clamp digits are two thirds of committed digits, this is the only item that could remove a double-digit number of the 25 s.
   - Unknown: proof for virtual vectors (commit-before-Θ ordering); succinct Θ (start with a hash-expanded Θ and linear verifier work); slack ≈ 2^9–2^10 versus real value magnitudes (roadmap measurement 4); completeness-error budget and retry policy; cost of the extra degree-2 linking sumcheck per tensor.
2. **Mosaic-style single commitment with free claim lifting (or upstream's one-native-group layout).**
   - Applies to: claim/opening reduction.
   - Saving: targets the 6.7 s opening stage and proof size; magnitude unmeasured. Upstream precedent: PR #2012.
   - Unknown: Akita's cost for one large sparse polynomial versus many small ones; interaction with bucket packing already in place.
3. **RA-virtualization geometry and kernels: four-factor virtual selectors, balanced product tree with finite-difference extension, forward-difference interpolation; then the 2026/587 product algorithm.**
   - Applies to: high-degree ra-virtualization (degree 9/17 instances that survive roadmap B/C).
   - Saving: upstream measured −38 to −44% on RA-virtualization spans from the geometry change alone; 2026/587 claims 1.7–2.2x on the high-degree Shout sumcheck.
   - Unknown: share of the 5.3 s IOP spent there today; 2026/587's algorithm details (paper not retrieved); geometry change alters proof format.
4. **Pattern/histogram Booleanity kernels (prover-only).**
   - Applies to: Booleanity over K = 16 one-hot digits.
   - Saving: upstream reports first four rounds from 16-row patterns and −11 to −16% on Booleanity messages for the caching variant; end-to-end small.
   - Unknown: whether jolt-atlas' digit layout admits the same block patterns.
5. **Deferred-reduction accumulation everywhere (already roadmap A1/A2), by bumping vendored kernels where shared.**
   - Applies to: matmul fold and rounds, element-wise products, opening reduction.
   - Saving: upstream stage-level 3–12%; roadmap estimates 2–4x on the fold from this plus memory order.
   - Unknown: none protocol-level; pure engineering.
6. **Fold the Hamming-weight-one check into the read-raf sumcheck with a random constant term (BinaryShout's α₀).**
   - Applies to: table lookups (read-only).
   - Saving: one sumcheck instance per lookup family; small.
   - Unknown: whether the existing claim-reduction design already amortises this.
7. **Dense bounded commitment for small values that need no one-hot address (upstream PR #2145 pattern).**
   - Applies to: range checks/commitment, only where a value's range is implied elsewhere or by the commitment.
   - Saving: upstream removed 17 one-hot columns for increments. For jolt-atlas it is the enabling primitive for research-directions §5 option B.
   - Unknown: whether the commitment's bound is extractable and exact (roadmap D3); this is the gating research question.
8. **Small-value sumcheck with round batching (ℓ0 ≈ 2–3) for `eq · small · small` relations.**
   - Applies to: element-wise products of small values (Mul, Square, Cube, softmax products).
   - Saving: likely < 2x on those instances on Fp128 (κ ≈ 9, eq factor present); they are a minority of a 5.3 s stage.
   - Unknown: real κ for the Solinas field; do after 1–5.
9. **Streaming first rounds for memory, not speed.**
   - Applies to: the largest sumchecks at long context.
   - Saving: about 2x table memory on the affected instance upstream (open PRs); time overhead claimed < 2x in theory.
   - Unknown: whether memory is a binding constraint for jolt-atlas before layer-wise chunking.

Do not adopt / park:
- **8-bit digits (roadmap D1)**: upstream measured K = 256 commit at 1.9x the K = 16 cost on Akita and moved to K = 16.
- **LogUp-GKR / LogUp\* range checks (roadmap D4)**: costs log₂N bits of soundness over Fp128 and still needs a dense small-value commitment; revisit only together with item 7.
- **Univariate skip in the prime field**: works upstream for Spartan's fixed relation, but 2025/1117 argues the small-value prover dominates it for small integers in large prime fields, and it is a protocol change with a known sharp edge.
- **Magnetar's Frobenius one-hot check, ring switching, binary-field skip codes**: characteristic-2 only.
- **Wholesale constraint-system rewrite**: no benefit for matmul; watch the upstream binary-field/LaBinius track instead.

### Gaps
- Every saving above is an estimate against un-profiled per-op costs; roadmap §2's measurements (digit census by producing op, value histograms, long-context run) decide ranks 1, 3 and 8.
- Items 1 and 7 each rest on an unproved claim (virtual-vector approximate range soundness; extractable bounds from Akita's dense commitment).
